quickAnswer: "Blot (don't rub) excess coffee immediately, then treat with a mix of cool water, a few drops of dish soap, and white vinegar, working from the outside of the stain inward. Most fresh stains lift fully within 10–15 minutes."
symptoms:
  - "Brown discoloration on carpet fibers, fresh or dried"
  - "A ring-shaped mark where a spilled coffee puddle dried unevenly"
  - "Lingering coffee smell even after the visible stain fades"
causes:
  - title: "Tannins in coffee bonding to carpet fibers"
    description: "Coffee contains tannins, the same staining compounds found in tea and wine, which bond to fibers quickly, especially if left to dry."
  - title: "Rubbing instead of blotting on first contact"
    description: "Rubbing a fresh spill pushes it deeper into the fibers and spreads it outward, making it harder to fully remove."
  - title: "Cream or sugar in the coffee"
    description: "Added cream or sugar can leave a sticky residue behind even after the brown color is treated, requiring an extra rinse step."
safeChecks:
  - "Test any cleaning solution on a hidden corner of carpet first to confirm it doesn't affect the color."
  - "Check whether the carpet is wool, synthetic, or a blend, since wool needs a gentler approach with cooler water."
  - "Confirm the stain is fully dry or still wet, since the technique differs slightly for each."
steps:
  - title: "Blot up excess liquid"
    description: "For a fresh spill, press a clean white cloth or paper towel firmly onto the stain, working from the outer edge inward, to absorb as much coffee as possible before it sets."
  - title: "Mix the cleaning solution"
    description: "Combine 1 cup of cool water, 1 teaspoon of dish soap, and 1 tablespoon of white vinegar in a bowl."
  - title: "Apply and blot"
    description: "Dab the solution onto the stain with a clean cloth, then blot (don't scrub) to lift the color. Repeat, using a fresh part of the cloth each time to avoid redepositing the stain."
  - title: "Rinse with plain water"
    description: "Dab with a cloth dampened in plain cool water to remove any soap residue, then blot dry."
  - title: "Dry and fluff"
    description: "Press a dry towel over the area to absorb remaining moisture, then let it air dry fully before walking on it or fluffing the fibers with your fingers."
dontDo:
  - "Don't use hot water on a coffee stain — heat can set the tannins permanently into the fibers."
  - "Don't scrub aggressively — this spreads the stain and can damage carpet fibers."
  - "Don't use bleach-based cleaners on colored carpet without testing an inconspicuous spot first."
whenToCallPro:
  - "The stain is on a wool, silk, or other delicate specialty rug rather than standard carpet."
  - "The stain has set for weeks or is a large area and household methods aren't lifting it."
  - "You notice discoloration or texture changes in the carpet after attempting to clean it."
faqs:
  - q: "Does this method work on dried, old coffee stains?"
    a: "Yes, though it may take a couple of repeated applications. Gently dampen the dried stain first to loosen it before applying the cleaning solution."
  - q: "Can I use hydrogen peroxide instead of vinegar?"
    a: "On light-colored carpets, a small amount of hydrogen peroxide can be effective, but always test on a hidden area first since it has mild bleaching potential."
  - q: "What if the coffee had milk or cream in it?"
    a: "Follow the same steps, but do a final rinse pass with plain water to make sure no sticky residue is left behind, which can otherwise attract dirt."
