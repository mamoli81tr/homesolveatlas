quickAnswer: "Surface wiping rarely removes mold from silicone sealant because it grows into small pores in the material. A bleach-gel or diluted bleach soak, left in contact for 15–30 minutes, gets much better results than spray cleaners."
symptoms:
  - "Black, gray, or pink spots along shower or bathtub silicone seams"
  - "Discoloration that returns within days of surface cleaning"
  - "A musty smell near the affected sealant"
  - "Spots that seem to grow slightly beneath the surface of the silicone"
causes:
  - title: "Trapped moisture in a warm, humid environment"
    description: "Bathrooms combine consistent moisture, warmth, and organic residue (soap, skin cells) — ideal conditions for mold growth."
  - title: "Poor ventilation"
    description: "Without an extractor fan or open window used regularly after showers, humidity lingers on surfaces far longer than needed."
  - title: "Mold growing within the silicone itself"
    description: "Silicone sealant has a slightly porous surface that mold spores can colonize below the visible surface, which is why surface wiping alone often doesn't fully remove it."
safeChecks:
  - "Ventilate the bathroom well — open a window or run the extractor fan — before using any bleach-based product."
  - "Check that the silicone itself is still in good, unbroken condition rather than cracked or peeling away from the surface."
  - "Confirm no one in the household has a bleach sensitivity or respiratory condition that the fumes could aggravate."
steps:
  - title: "Ventilate and prep the area"
    description: "Open a window or turn on the extractor fan, and clear the shower or tub of any bottles or items."
  - title: "Apply a bleach gel or diluted bleach solution"
    description: "Use a bleach-based mold gel product, or mix 1 part household bleach to 4 parts water, and apply along the moldy seam using a small brush or cloth."
  - title: "Let it sit"
    description: "Leave the solution in contact with the mold for 15–30 minutes (check the product label for exact timing) — this is what surface cleaners skip, and it's the key to actually killing mold below the surface."
  - title: "Scrub gently and rinse"
    description: "Use an old toothbrush to gently work along the seam, then rinse thoroughly with water."
  - title: "Dry and monitor"
    description: "Wipe the area dry and keep it ventilated. Check back over the following week to see if any staining reappears."
dontDo:
  - "Don't mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaning products — this produces dangerous fumes."
  - "Don't scrub with abrasive pads that can tear or degrade the silicone sealant."
  - "Don't skip ventilation when using bleach-based products in a small bathroom."
whenToCallPro:
  - "Mold keeps returning within days despite consistent treatment and improved ventilation."
  - "The silicone is cracked, peeling, or pulling away from the tile — it likely needs to be stripped out and fully re-caulked."
  - "Mold covers a large area beyond just the sealant seams, which may indicate a broader moisture or ventilation problem."
  - "Anyone in the household has asthma or a mold allergy and symptoms worsen near the affected area."
faqs:
  - q: "Will regular bathroom cleaner remove mold from silicone?"
    a: "It can remove surface discoloration temporarily, but mold that has grown into the sealant's pores usually returns within days without a longer-contact treatment like a bleach gel or soak."
  - q: "How do I stop mold from coming back?"
    a: "Improve ventilation (run the extractor fan for 20–30 minutes after every shower), squeegee wet surfaces, and wipe down the sealant weekly."
  - q: "When should I just replace the silicone instead of cleaning it?"
    a: "If it's cracked, gapped, or mold has clearly grown throughout its full depth rather than just staining the surface, removing and re-caulking is more effective than repeated cleaning."
