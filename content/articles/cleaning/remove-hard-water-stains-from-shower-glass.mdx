quickAnswer: "Most cloudy shower glass is mineral scale from hard water, which dissolves with an acidic cleaner like white vinegar given enough contact time — no abrasive scrubbing needed. Glass that stays cloudy after a proper vinegar soak may have permanent mineral etching instead."
symptoms:
  - "A white or cloudy haze covering the glass, worse near the bottom"
  - "Spots that feel slightly rough or gritty to the touch"
  - "Haze that's more visible when the glass is dry than when wet"
  - "Streaks that follow where water naturally runs down the door"
causes:
  - title: "Mineral scale from hard water"
    description: "Calcium and magnesium in hard water leave a mineral film behind as water evaporates on the glass, building up layer by layer over weeks and months without regular squeegeeing."
  - title: "Soap scum combined with mineral deposits"
    description: "Soap residue combines with hard-water minerals to form a stickier, harder-to-remove film than either would create alone."
  - title: "Infrequent cleaning or squeegeeing"
    description: "Without a quick wipe-down after each shower, mineral deposits have time to dry and bond to the glass surface repeatedly."
  - title: "Permanent etching (less common)"
    description: "In severe, long-neglected cases, mineral deposits can very gradually etch into the glass surface itself, which cleaning can reduce in appearance but not fully reverse."
steps:
  - title: "Soak with white vinegar"
    description: "Soak paper towels or a cloth in undiluted white vinegar and press them against the glass, or spray the glass generously, and let it sit for 15–30 minutes so the acid has time to dissolve the mineral scale."
  - title: "Scrub gently with a non-abrasive pad"
    description: "After soaking, scrub gently in circular motions with a soft sponge or non-scratch pad — avoid steel wool or abrasive scrubbers, which can scratch glass."
  - title: "Rinse thoroughly"
    description: "Rinse the glass fully with water to remove all vinegar residue and loosened mineral film."
  - title: "Dry and squeegee"
    description: "Dry with a microfiber cloth and get in the habit of squeegeeing the glass after every shower going forward — this single habit prevents most future buildup."
  - title: "Repeat or use a stronger product for stubborn buildup"
    description: "For heavy, long-neglected buildup, a second vinegar soak or a commercial hard-water stain remover (following its instructions) may be needed."
safeChecks:
  - "Test any cleaner on a small corner of the glass first, especially if it's tinted or has a coating."
  - "Check whether the haze feels smooth (likely just scale) or rough/pitted (possible etching) to set realistic expectations before starting."
  - "Ventilate the bathroom well when using vinegar, especially in a small space."
dontDo:
  - "Don't use steel wool, razor blades, or other abrasive tools on glass — they can scratch the surface permanently."
  - "Don't mix vinegar with bleach — this produces harmful fumes."
  - "Don't skip the post-shower squeegee habit after cleaning — without it, the same buildup will simply return over the following weeks."
whenToCallPro:
  - "The haze doesn't improve after a proper vinegar soak and gentle scrub, suggesting permanent etching rather than removable scale."
  - "You want a protective glass coating applied to reduce future mineral buildup — this is a service some glass and bathroom specialists offer."
faqs:
  - q: "Does a squeegee really make that much difference?"
    a: "Yes — most hard-water buildup happens because water is left to evaporate on the glass. A 10-second squeegee after each shower removes the water before it can leave mineral deposits, which prevents the vast majority of future buildup."
  - q: "Is vinegar safe on all shower glass?"
    a: "It's safe on standard tempered glass, but test a small area first if your glass has a special coating or tint, since some specialty coatings can be affected by acidic cleaners over repeated use."
  - q: "How can I tell if it's scale or permanent etching?"
    a: "Scale typically feels smooth or slightly gritty and dissolves with a proper vinegar soak; etching feels rough or pitted even when wet and doesn't fully clear no matter how long you soak it."
