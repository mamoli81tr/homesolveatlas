quickAnswer: "Sticky cabinet grease, especially near the stove, comes from airborne cooking oil settling on surfaces over time. A warm water and dish soap solution cuts through most buildup; for heavier grease, adding a small amount of baking soda or a degreaser makes a real difference without damaging the finish."
symptoms:
  - "A tacky or sticky feeling when you touch cabinet surfaces, especially near the stove"
  - "A dulled or slightly yellowed appearance on cabinet doors closest to cooking areas"
  - "Dust and lint sticking more than usual to cabinet surfaces"
  - "Grease buildup worse on cabinets directly above or beside the range"
causes:
  - title: "Airborne cooking oil settling over time"
    description: "Frying, sautéing, and even simmering release tiny oil particles into the air, which settle on nearby surfaces and slowly build up into a sticky film without regular wiping."
  - title: "Lack of ventilation while cooking"
    description: "Not using a range hood or exhaust fan while cooking lets more airborne grease settle in the kitchen instead of being vented outside."
  - title: "Infrequent cleaning near the stove specifically"
    description: "Cabinets farther from the stove often stay cleaner far longer, showing how concentrated the buildup is nearest the cooking source."
steps:
  - title: "Mix a warm soapy solution"
    description: "Combine warm water with a few drops of dish soap (which is specifically designed to break down grease) in a bucket or spray bottle."
  - title: "Wipe down with a soft cloth"
    description: "Apply the solution to a microfiber cloth (not directly on wood cabinets, to avoid oversaturating) and wipe in the direction of the wood grain, rinsing the cloth frequently."
  - title: "Add baking soda for heavier buildup"
    description: "For stubborn grease, make a paste of baking soda and a little water, apply it to the greasy area, let it sit for a few minutes, then wipe away with a damp cloth."
  - title: "Dry thoroughly"
    description: "Wipe cabinets dry with a clean cloth afterward — leaving moisture sitting on wood cabinet surfaces can damage the finish over time."
  - title: "Address ventilation going forward"
    description: "Use your range hood or exhaust fan every time you cook, especially when frying, to significantly slow how quickly grease re-accumulates."
safeChecks:
  - "Test any cleaning solution on a hidden corner or inside edge of a cabinet first, especially with painted or specialty finishes."
  - "Check whether cabinets are a sealed finish (safe for a damp cloth) or unfinished/unsealed wood (needs a much drier approach)."
  - "Avoid excess water on any wood cabinet — a damp, not wet, cloth is the safer standard."
dontDo:
  - "Don't use abrasive scouring pads on painted or finished cabinet surfaces — they can dull or scratch the finish."
  - "Don't soak wood cabinets with water — excess moisture can cause swelling, warping, or finish damage over time."
  - "Don't use strong ammonia-based cleaners on cabinets with a specific manufacturer-recommended cleaning product, since they can strip some finishes."
whenToCallPro:
  - "Grease buildup has caused visible finish damage (dulling, warping, or peeling) that cleaning alone won't fix."
  - "You're dealing with cabinets that have a specialty or antique finish and want to avoid any risk of damage from DIY cleaning."
faqs:
  - q: "How often should I wipe down cabinets near the stove?"
    a: "A quick wipe every week or two for cabinets closest to the stove prevents grease from building into a stubborn, harder-to-remove layer, compared to letting it accumulate for months."
  - q: "Is dish soap really enough, or do I need a specialty degreaser?"
    a: "Dish soap handles most everyday buildup well since it's formulated specifically to break down grease. A dedicated kitchen degreaser can help with heavier, longer-neglected buildup, but always check it's safe for your cabinet finish first."
  - q: "Does using a range hood actually reduce cabinet grease buildup?"
    a: "Yes significantly — venting cooking oil particles outside rather than letting them settle in the kitchen is one of the most effective ways to slow future buildup on all nearby surfaces, not just cabinets."
