causes:
  - title: "Bigger isn't automatically better"

  - title: "Undersizing leads to constant running"
    description: "A unit that's too small for the space runs continuously trying to reach the set temperature, using more energy and wearing out faster."
  - title: "Room-specific factors change the ideal size"
    description: "Sun exposure, ceiling height, number of regular occupants, and whether the room includes a kitchen all shift the actual BTU need up or down from the base estimate."
steps:
  - title: "Measure the room's area"
    description: "Measure length and width, then multiply to get square footage (or square meters)."
  - title: "Apply the base rule"
    description: "Multiply the area by about 20 BTU per square foot as a starting baseline."
  - title: "Adjust for sun exposure"
    description: "Increase by about 10% for a consistently sunny, south-facing room; decrease by about 10% for a shaded or north-facing room."
  - title: "Adjust for occupants and kitchen"
    description: "Add roughly 600 BTU for each regular occupant beyond two, and add about 4,000 BTU if the room includes a kitchen."
  - title: "Choose a unit within that range"
    description: "Pick a unit whose rated BTU falls within your calculated range rather than always rounding up to the next size."
safeChecks:

dontDo:
  - "Don't assume a bigger unit is always better — oversizing leads to poor humidity control and short-cycling, which wastes energy and shortens the unit's lifespan."

  - "Don't use whole-home square footage for a single-room window or portable unit calculation — size it to the specific room being cooled."
