quickAnswer: "A musty AC smell is almost always mold or mildew growing somewhere damp in the system — most often the evaporator coil or a clogged condensate drain line, both of which stay wet during normal operation and can grow mold if not properly draining or ventilating."
symptoms:
  - "A musty, mildew-like smell when the AC first turns on"
  - "Smell fades after the unit has run for a while, then returns at startup"
  - "Visible water near the indoor unit or a musty smell strongest near the vents"
  - "Increased allergy or respiratory symptoms when the AC runs"
causes:
  - title: "Mold or mildew on the evaporator coil"
    description: "The evaporator coil stays cold and wet while cooling, which is an ideal environment for mold if airflow and drainage aren't working properly."
  - title: "A clogged condensate drain line"
    description: "The line that carries condensation away from the unit can clog with algae and debris, causing standing water and a musty smell, sometimes with visible dripping."
  - title: "A dirty air filter"
    description: "A filter overdue for replacement restricts airflow, keeping the coil wetter for longer after each cooling cycle and encouraging mold growth."
  - title: "Poor drainage in ductwork"
    description: "In humid climates, condensation can occasionally collect within ductwork itself if insulation or sealing is inadequate."
safeChecks:
  - "Check your air filter's condition and replace it if it looks visibly dirty or hasn't been changed on schedule."
  - "Look near the indoor unit or the condensate drain line's exit point for standing water or a musty smell."
  - "Turn off the AC at the thermostat before doing any hands-on inspection of the unit."
steps:
  - title: "Replace the air filter"
    description: "A clean filter improves airflow and reduces how much moisture lingers on the coil after each cycle."
  - title: "Clear the condensate drain line"
    description: "Locate the drain line (often a PVC pipe near the indoor unit) and, with the system off, use a wet/dry vacuum at the outdoor end to pull out any algae clog, or pour a cup of distilled white vinegar down the indoor access point to break down buildup."
  - title: "Inspect the evaporator coil if accessible"
    description: "Some systems have an accessible coil panel; visible mold there is best treated with an AC-safe no-rinse coil cleaner rather than household cleaners."
  - title: "Run the fan-only setting to dry the system"
    description: "Running the fan without cooling for 30 minutes after use helps dry residual moisture on the coil, reducing mold growth between cycles."
dontDo:
  - "Don't spray household bleach or general cleaners directly onto the evaporator coil — use a product specifically labeled safe for AC coils."
  - "Don't ignore a persistent musty smell, especially if anyone in the household has asthma or allergies — indoor mold can affect air quality."
  - "Don't pour anything down the drain line while the system is running."
whenToCallPro:
  - "The musty smell persists after replacing the filter and clearing the drain line."
  - "You see visible mold growth on the coil itself or inside ductwork."
  - "There's active water leaking from the indoor unit rather than just a smell."
  - "Household members experience worsening allergy or respiratory symptoms specifically when the AC runs."
faqs:
  - q: "Is a musty AC smell dangerous?"
    a: "It's a sign of mold growth in the system, which can affect indoor air quality over time, particularly for people with asthma or mold allergies — it's worth addressing rather than ignoring, even if it's not an emergency."
  - q: "How often should I clear the condensate drain line?"
    a: "Once or twice per cooling season is a reasonable preventive schedule, more often in humid climates where algae growth is faster."
  - q: "Will running the AC on fan-only help or make it worse?"
    a: "It helps — running the fan alone after cooling cycles helps dry the coil, which reduces the damp conditions mold needs to grow."
