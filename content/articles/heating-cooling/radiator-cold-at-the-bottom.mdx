quickAnswer: "Cold at the bottom, warm at the top is the classic sign of sludge (rust and mineral debris) settled at the base of the radiator, blocking water flow through the lower section. A power flush or radiator removal-and-flush usually resolves it."
symptoms:
  - "Top of the radiator is warm while the bottom stays noticeably cold"
  - "The radiator takes longer than others in the house to heat up"
  - "A gurgling sound from the radiator or nearby pipes"
  - "Uneven heating throughout the room compared to before"
causes:
  - title: "Sludge buildup at the base"
    description: "Over years, rust particles and mineral debris circulate through a heating system and settle at the lowest points, which is usually the bottom of each radiator."
  - title: "Trapped air (less likely for this specific pattern)"
    description: "Trapped air typically causes the opposite pattern — cold at the top, warm at the bottom — but is worth ruling out with a bleed check regardless."
  - title: "A closing or partially blocked valve"
    description: "If the radiator's flow or return valve is only partly open, water circulation through the whole unit can be restricted unevenly."
safeChecks:
  - "Confirm the pattern: cold at the bottom with warm at the top points to sludge, while the reverse points to trapped air."
  - "Check that both the flow valve and the lockshield (return) valve at the radiator's base are fully open."
  - "Feel the radiator at several heights to map out exactly where the cold section starts."
steps:
  - title: "Rule out trapped air first"
    description: "Use a radiator bleed key on the small valve at the top corner to release any trapped air — if the pattern was actually air-related, this alone may fix it."
  - title: "Check both valves are open"
    description: "Confirm the manual valve and the lockshield valve at the base of the radiator are both fully open, not partially closed."
  - title: "Try a simple flush if sludge is suspected"
    description: "For a single problem radiator, turning off both valves, disconnecting it, and flushing it out over a bath or outdoors with a hose can clear moderate sludge."
  - title: "Reconnect and test"
    description: "Reattach the radiator, reopen both valves slowly, bleed any air introduced during reconnection, and check that heat now reaches the full height evenly."
  - title: "Consider a whole-system power flush for multiple radiators"
    description: "If several radiators in the house show the same cold-bottom pattern, sludge is likely widespread through the system, and a professional power flush is more effective than treating radiators individually."
dontDo:
  - "Don't disconnect a radiator from a pressurized central heating system without first turning off both valves and understanding how to safely relieve pressure."
  - "Don't add chemical sludge remover to the system without following the product's instructions on dosage and circulation time."
  - "Don't ignore worsening cold spots across multiple radiators — this typically gets worse, not better, without a flush."
whenToCallPro:
  - "Multiple radiators in the home show the same sludge pattern, suggesting system-wide buildup that needs a professional power flush."
  - "You're not confident safely draining, disconnecting, or reconnecting a radiator on a pressurized system."
  - "The boiler itself is also making unusual noises, which can indicate sludge has reached it too."
  - "You have a combi or pressurized system and are unsure how to safely relieve pressure before working on a radiator."
faqs:
  - q: "How is cold-at-the-bottom different from cold-at-the-top?"
    a: "Cold at the top usually means trapped air, fixed by bleeding the radiator. Cold at the bottom usually means sludge settled at the base, which bleeding won't fix — it needs a flush."
  - q: "How often do radiators need a power flush?"
    a: "There's no fixed schedule, but if you notice several radiators developing cold spots, or your system is over 10 years old and has never been flushed, it's worth having assessed."
  - q: "Can I prevent sludge from building up again?"
    a: "Adding a chemical inhibitor to the system after a flush, and having your heating system serviced annually, both help slow sludge buildup over time."
