quickAnswer: "A dryer needing extra cycles is almost always an airflow problem — a full lint filter, a clogged exhaust vent, or overloading the drum are the three most common causes, roughly in that order."
symptoms:
  - "Clothes are still damp at the end of a normal cycle"
  - "The dryer runs noticeably hotter to the touch than usual"
  - "A burning-dust smell during operation"
  - "Cycles are taking much longer than the displayed estimated time"
causes:
  - title: "Full lint filter"
    description: "A lint screen that isn't cleaned before every load blocks airflow, which is the single most common cause of poor drying performance."
  - title: "Clogged exhaust vent"
    description: "Lint accumulates inside the rigid or flexible exhaust duct over months of use, especially in longer vent runs with several bends."
  - title: "Overloaded drum"
    description: "Packing the drum too full prevents clothes from tumbling freely, so hot air can't reach every item evenly."
  - title: "Faulty moisture sensor"
    description: "On sensor-dry cycles, a dirty or failing moisture sensor strip can end the cycle early even though clothes aren't fully dry."
safeChecks:
  - "Clean the lint filter before and after every load, not just occasionally."
  - "Check that the outside vent flap opens freely and isn't blocked by debris, snow, or a bird's nest."
  - "Reduce load size and re-run a cycle to see if drying time improves significantly."
  - "Wipe the moisture sensor strips (usually two small metal bars inside the drum) with a damp cloth to remove residue."
steps:
  - title: "Clean the lint filter thoroughly"
    description: "Remove all visible lint, then wash the screen with warm soapy water occasionally to clear the fabric-softener film that lint alone doesn't show."
  - title: "Inspect the full vent run"
    description: "Disconnect the vent hose from the back of the dryer and check for lint buildup at both ends and any sagging sections where lint can settle."
  - title: "Clear the vent"
    description: "Use a vent-cleaning brush kit or a shop vacuum to clear lint from the full duct length, including the exterior wall cap."
  - title: "Right-size your loads"
    description: "Split larger loads into two smaller ones — clothes need room to tumble for even, efficient drying."
  - title: "Run a test cycle"
    description: "Time a normal load after cleaning to confirm drying time has returned to the expected range for your model."
dontDo:
  - "Don't run the dryer with a damaged or missing lint filter — this pushes lint further into the vent system and increases fire risk."
  - "Don't use flexible plastic vent hose for exhaust runs — it's a fire hazard and is banned in many areas; use rigid or semi-rigid metal duct."
  - "Don't ignore a burning smell — stop the dryer immediately and investigate."
whenToCallPro:
  - "The vent has been cleaned and loads are still taking two cycles."
  - "You notice a persistent burning smell even after clearing lint."
  - "The vent run is long, has many bends, or runs through the attic and you're not confident clearing it yourself."
  - "The dryer shuts off mid-cycle repeatedly, which can indicate a failing thermal safety fuse."
faqs:
  - q: "How often should I clean my dryer vent?"
    a: "Once a year for average use is the general guideline, more often for households doing frequent laundry or with long/complex vent runs."
  - q: "Does overloading really make that much difference?"
    a: "Yes — clothes need to tumble through the airflow to dry evenly. An overpacked drum traps damp fabric in the center where hot air barely reaches."
  - q: "Can a clogged vent be a fire risk?"
    a: "Yes. Lint is highly flammable, and a blocked vent traps heat inside the dryer and duct, which is one of the leading causes of home dryer fires."
