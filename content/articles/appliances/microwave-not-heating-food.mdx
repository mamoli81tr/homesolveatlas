quickAnswer: "If the display lights up, the turntable spins, and the timer counts down normally but food stays cold, the magnetron (the component that actually generates microwave energy) has very likely failed. A faulty door switch or a blown high-voltage fuse are the other common causes."
symptoms:
  - "Display, light, and turntable all work normally"
  - "Food comes out at the same temperature it went in"
  - "No humming sound that's normally audible while microwaving"
  - "The problem started suddenly rather than gradually"
causes:
  - title: "Failed magnetron"
    description: "The magnetron generates the microwave energy that actually heats food. It's a sealed component that simply wears out after years of use, and when it fails, everything else in the microwave keeps working normally except heating."
  - title: "Faulty door switch (interlock)"
    description: "Microwaves have multiple safety switches that must all confirm the door is closed before allowing the magnetron to run. If one has failed, the unit can appear to run normally while the magnetron is prevented from activating."
  - title: "Blown high-voltage fuse"
    description: "This fuse protects the high-voltage circuit that powers the magnetron. If it's blown, the rest of the microwave can operate normally while the heating circuit is completely dead."
  - title: "Failed high-voltage diode or capacitor"
    description: "These components convert power for the magnetron. A failure here can also stop heating while leaving the display, light, and turntable unaffected."
safeChecks:
  - "Confirm the turntable, light, and display all function normally, which helps narrow the fault to the heating circuit specifically."
  - "Test with a mug of water for no more than 30 seconds — if it's not even slightly warm afterward, this confirms a heating fault rather than a weak/uneven heat issue."
  - "Listen for the characteristic humming sound during operation — its absence supports a magnetron or high-voltage circuit fault."
  - "Do not open the microwave's outer casing yourself under any circumstances — see the safety warning below."
steps:
  - title: "Confirm the fault with a simple water test"
    description: "Microwave a mug of water for 30 seconds and check if it's warmed at all — no change in temperature confirms a heating-circuit fault rather than a minor performance issue."
  - title: "Check the door is latching fully"
    description: "Open and firmly close the door, confirming it latches securely — a door that doesn't latch fully can prevent the magnetron from running as a safety measure."
  - title: "Stop using the unit and unplug it"
    description: "Once a heating fault is confirmed, unplug the microwave and don't continue using it — running a countertop microwave with a magnetron or high-voltage fault isn't something to keep testing at home."
  - title: "Arrange professional diagnosis or replacement"
    description: "Have a qualified appliance technician diagnose which specific high-voltage component has failed, or weigh repair cost against replacement — magnetron replacement is often comparable in cost to a new mid-range microwave."
dontDo:
  - "Don't open the microwave's outer casing yourself — the high-voltage capacitor can hold a dangerous electrical charge even after the unit is unplugged."
  - "Don't keep running the microwave repeatedly 'to test it' once you've confirmed it isn't heating — this doesn't help diagnose the issue and risks further damage."
  - "Don't attempt to test or discharge the high-voltage capacitor yourself without proper training and tools."
whenToCallPro:

  - "You're deciding between repair and replacement — a technician can give you a repair quote to compare against a new unit's cost."
faqs:
  - q: "Is it worth repairing a microwave that won't heat?"
    a: "It depends on the unit's age and the repair quote — magnetron replacement can cost a meaningful fraction of a new microwave's price, so for an older unit, replacement is often more practical than repair."
  - q: "Can I test the magnetron myself?"
    a: "This isn't recommended. Diagnosing the magnetron and related high-voltage components requires specific test equipment and safety knowledge due to the capacitor's stored charge, even when the unit is unplugged."
  - q: "Why does everything else still work if the magnetron failed?"
    a: "The display, turntable motor, and light run on entirely separate low-voltage circuits from the high-voltage circuit that powers the magnetron, so one can fail while the others keep working normally."
