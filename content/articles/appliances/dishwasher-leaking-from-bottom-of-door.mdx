quickAnswer: "Water appearing at the base of the door during a cycle, rather than staying contained in the tub, usually means the door seal (gasket) has worn out or is dirty enough to prevent a full seal, the machine is oversudsing from the wrong detergent, or it's being overfilled. Because this leak happens near the base of the machine — close to the power cord and outlet — treat it as a stop-and-investigate situation rather than something to let run its course."
symptoms:
  - "Water visible pooling on the floor directly in front of the dishwasher during or right after a cycle"
  - "The leak may be worse partway through, especially during wash or spray-heavy stages"
  - "Suds may be visible in the leaked water"
  - "The kickplate or cabinet base may feel damp even without an obvious puddle"
causes:
  - title: "Worn or dirty door seal"
    description: "The rubber gasket around the door can lose its shape, crack, or accumulate enough food debris over time to stop sealing fully against the tub."
  - title: "Oversudsing from the wrong detergent"
    description: "Regular dish soap or too much detergent produces excess foam that can escape past the door seal even when the seal itself is in reasonable condition."
  - title: "Overfilling"
    description: "A float switch malfunction or an overly full water level can raise water high enough in the tub to reach and escape past the door seal during wash agitation."
  - title: "Door not latching or aligning correctly"
    description: "A door that's slightly misaligned, or a latch not pulling it fully snug against the seal, can leave a gap water finds its way through under spray pressure."
safeChecks:
  - "Stop the cycle and, once safe, check whether suds are present in the leaked water, pointing toward detergent as a contributing cause."
  - "With the machine off and door open, run a finger around the full door seal checking for tears, flattening, or trapped debris."
  - "Check that the door closes flush and latches firmly with no visible gap or looseness."
  - "Unplug the dishwasher before any closer inspection or cleaning near the base."
steps:
  - title: "Stop the cycle if a leak is actively happening"
    description: "Cancel or pause the cycle rather than letting it continue, given the leak's proximity to the base of the machine and nearby electrical connections."
  - title: "Clean the door seal thoroughly"
    description: "With the machine unplugged, wipe down the entire gasket to remove food debris and residue buildup that could be preventing a full seal."
  - title: "Switch detergent if oversudsing is suspected"
    description: "Confirm you're using dishwasher-specific detergent at the correct dose, not liquid dish soap or an excessive amount of the correct product."
  - title: "Inspect door alignment and latch"
    description: "Check that the door sits flush and latches firmly — a door that's slightly warped or a latch that's loosened may need adjustment or replacement."
dontDo:
  - "Don't ignore a door leak hoping it will stop on its own — water pooling repeatedly at the base of the machine, near its power connection, is worth addressing promptly rather than living with."
  - "Don't use regular dish soap in a dishwasher — its foam can escape past seals and is a common, easily fixed cause of exactly this symptom."
  - "Don't run the machine again immediately after a leak without at least checking the seal and detergent, since the same leak will likely recur."
whenToCallPro:
  - "The door seal shows visible damage and needs replacing."
  - "The leak continues after cleaning the seal, confirming correct detergent, and checking door alignment."
  - "You're not comfortable determining whether water has reached anywhere near the machine's electrical connections."
  - "The door itself appears warped or won't align and latch properly."
faqs:

  - q: "Can I just keep a towel down and keep using the machine?"

  - q: "Is this the same as water pooling inside the tub at the bottom?"
    a: "No — that's usually a drainage issue with water staying inside the machine, covered in our standing-water guide. This is specifically about water escaping past the door to the floor outside the machine, which has a different set of causes."

If you see water pooling near the outlet or power cord, do not touch the puddle or the machine — switch off power at the breaker (not just the outlet) before investigating further, and contact a qualified technician if you're at all unsure."

Because this leak happens at the door rather than inside the tub, it's a different problem from standing water at the bottom — and because of where it ends up on the floor, it deserves a more cautious, stop-first approach than most dishwasher symptoms.

| What you notice | Most likely cause |
|---|---|
| Suds visible in the leaked water | Oversudsing from wrong/too much detergent |
| Seal looks visibly worn, flattened, or torn | Worn door seal |
| Door doesn't sit flush or latch firmly | Door alignment or latch issue |
| Leak coincides with a fuller-than-usual water level | Overfilling |
