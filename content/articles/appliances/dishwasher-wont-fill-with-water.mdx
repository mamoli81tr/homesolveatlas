quickAnswer: "If the dishwasher powers on normally but never takes on water, check the simplest cause first: the water supply shutoff valve behind or under the sink may be closed, often after other under-sink work. A stuck float switch (which can falsely signal 'already full') or a faulty inlet valve are the next most common causes."
symptoms:
  - "Display and controls respond normally, but no filling sound follows starting a cycle"
  - "The cycle may pause or show a fill-related fault code"
  - "No water visible in the tub at any point"
  - "The problem may have started after plumbing work under the sink"
causes:
  - title: "Water supply shutoff valve closed"
    description: "The dedicated valve supplying the dishwasher, usually under the sink, can be closed accidentally during other plumbing work and simply never reopened."
  - title: "Float switch stuck in the 'up' (full) position"
    description: "A small float in the tub tells the machine when to stop filling — if it's stuck up (from being bumped or debris underneath it), the machine thinks it's already full and won't add water."
  - title: "Clogged inlet valve screen"
    description: "A small mesh screen where the water line enters the machine can clog with sediment over time, restricting or fully blocking incoming water."
  - title: "Failed water inlet valve"
    description: "The solenoid valve itself can fail electrically or mechanically, which is a less common but real possibility once supply and float switch are ruled out."
safeChecks:
  - "Check the water shutoff valve under the sink, confirming it's turned fully to open."
  - "Check the float (a small dome or cylinder on the tub floor) to see if it's stuck up or has debris trapped beneath it."
  - "Run another sink or appliance to confirm your home's water supply itself is working normally."
  - "Unplug the dishwasher before doing any closer inspection near the water inlet."
steps:
  - title: "Check the shutoff valve first"

  - title: "Inspect and free the float switch"
    description: "Gently lift and release the float to check it moves freely, and clear away any debris that might be trapping it in the raised position."
  - title: "Check for a fault code"
    description: "Check your manual for what any displayed code specifically indicates, which can point directly at the float switch or inlet valve."
  - title: "Note the pattern for service if it persists"
    description: "Whether the float moves freely and the supply valve is open are both useful details to have ready if you need a technician for the inlet valve itself."
dontDo:

  - "Don't force the float switch if it feels stuck rather than gently checking for an obvious obstruction."

faqs:
  - q: "Why would a shutoff valve just close on its own?"
    a: "It rarely closes on its own — this is most often the result of other under-sink plumbing work (like fixing a leak or installing a garbage disposal) where the valve was closed and not reopened afterward."
  - q: "How do I know if the float switch is the problem?"
    a: "Check it for free movement by hand — it should lift and drop back down smoothly. If it's stuck in the raised position or has debris trapped underneath, that's a strong sign it's telling the machine the tub is already full."
  - q: "Is this different from a dishwasher that fills but doesn't wash?"
    a: "Yes — this is about no water entering at all, which points to the supply, float switch, or inlet valve. A machine that fills normally but doesn't wash has a completely different fault in the wash pump or motor."

Because the display and controls work normally, the machine clearly has power — this narrows things down specifically to the water supply path, which has only a few realistic points of failure.

| What you notice | Most likely cause |
|---|---|
| Started after other plumbing work nearby | Water shutoff valve closed |
| Float doesn't move freely by hand | Stuck float switch |
| Everything checks out but still no water | Clogged or failed inlet valve |
| A specific fault code is displayed | Check your manual for the exact component it points to |
