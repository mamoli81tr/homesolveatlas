quickAnswer: "If the drum tumbles and the timer counts down normally but clothes come out cold and wet, the dryer is running without heat. A blown thermal fuse is the single most common cause, followed by a tripped circuit breaker (electric dryers use two) or a closed gas valve (gas dryers)."
symptoms:
  - "Drum tumbles normally and the cycle completes on schedule"
  - "Clothes come out cold, not warm, and just as wet as when loaded"
  - "No warm air felt at the lint trap opening or exhaust vent"
  - "The dryer may have recently overheated or had a badly clogged vent"
causes:
  - title: "Blown thermal fuse"
    description: "This safety fuse cuts power to the heating element if the dryer overheats, often due to a clogged vent restricting airflow. Once blown, it must be replaced — it doesn't reset itself."
  - title: "Tripped breaker (electric dryers)"
    description: "Electric dryers typically run on a 240V double-pole breaker. If only one of the two poles trips, the drum can still turn (120V) while the heating element (240V) gets no power."
  - title: "Closed gas supply valve (gas dryers)"
    description: "If the gas shutoff valve behind the dryer isn't fully open, the burner won't ignite even though the motor runs fine."
  - title: "Failed heating element or igniter"
    description: "The heating element (electric) or igniter (gas) itself can fail after years of use, independent of any fuse or supply issue."
safeChecks:
  - "Check your breaker panel for a tripped or partially tripped double-pole breaker (electric dryers)."
  - "Confirm the gas shutoff valve behind the dryer is turned fully to 'open' (gas dryers)."
  - "Feel for warm air at the lint trap opening partway through a cycle to confirm whether heat is present at all."
  - "Check when the vent was last cleaned — a badly clogged vent is a common cause of thermal-fuse failure."
steps:
  - title: "Check power and gas supply first"
    description: "Reset any tripped breaker (both poles for electric dryers) or confirm the gas valve is fully open, then run a short test cycle."
  - title: "Clear the vent"
    description: "Even if this isn't the direct cause, a clogged vent is what blows thermal fuses in the first place — clean the lint filter and the full exhaust vent run before doing anything else."
  - title: "Test for a blown thermal fuse"
    description: "This small safety fuse sits on or near the blower housing or heating element depending on the model. With power disconnected, a multimeter continuity test (or a technician) can confirm if it's blown."
  - title: "Replace the fuse and address the cause"
    description: "A blown thermal fuse is inexpensive to replace, but always clear the vent fully first — replacing the fuse without fixing airflow just sets up the next failure."
dontDo:
  - "Don't repeatedly reset a tripped breaker without investigating why it tripped — a 240V circuit tripping on a heating appliance can indicate a real fault."
  - "Don't attempt any gas line or gas valve repair yourself beyond confirming the shutoff valve position."
  - "Don't replace a thermal fuse without also clearing the vent — a blocked vent will blow the new fuse again."
whenToCallPro:
  - "You've confirmed power and gas supply are fine, and cleared the vent, but there's still no heat."
  - "You smell gas near a gas dryer at any point — leave the area and contact your gas utility immediately."
  - "The breaker keeps tripping again after being reset."
  - "You're not comfortable testing or replacing the thermal fuse or heating element yourself."
faqs:
  - q: "Why would only the heat stop working but the drum still turns?"
    a: "The drum motor and the heating circuit are separate systems in most dryers. A blown thermal fuse, a single tripped breaker pole, or a failed heating element can each cut heat specifically while leaving the motor unaffected."
  - q: "Does a clogged vent really cause this?"
    a: "Yes — restricted airflow traps heat inside the dryer, which is exactly the overheating condition the thermal fuse is designed to catch by cutting power. Clogged vents are the leading cause of thermal fuse failures."
  - q: "Is it safe to keep using the dryer with no heat while I wait to fix it?"
    a: "It's not unsafe, just ineffective — but don't ignore it for long if the cause is a blocked vent, since that also raises fire risk independent of the no-heat symptom."
sources:
