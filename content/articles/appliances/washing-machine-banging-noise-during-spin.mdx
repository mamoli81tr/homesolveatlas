quickAnswer: "A rhythmic shaking or 'walking' machine points to imbalance and leveling (see our shaking guide) — a distinct, repeated bang or knock, often once per rotation, more specifically suggests something is physically striking the tub: a loose counterweight, an object trapped in the door boot fold, or a drum support that's come loose."
symptoms:
  - "A distinct bang, thud, or knock — not a continuous shaking or vibration"
  - "The sound often repeats once per rotation, especially noticeable as spin speed changes"
  - "The rest of the cycle (wash, fill) is usually quiet and normal"
  - "The machine may or may not also shake, but the bang is the dominant, distinguishing symptom"
causes:
  - title: "Loose or broken counterweight"
    description: "Washers use concrete or metal counterweight blocks bolted to the tub to help balance it during spin. If a bolt loosens or a block cracks, it can shift and strike the cabinet or tub with each rotation."
  - title: "Object trapped in the door boot fold (front-loaders)"
    description: "Small items — coins, buttons, hair clips — can lodge in the fold of the rubber door gasket and knock against the drum or door glass as it spins."
  - title: "Loose or detached drum support/spider arm"
    description: "If the structural support connecting the drum to its drive shaft has loosened or partially detached, the drum can shift slightly and strike the tub with each rotation."
  - title: "Something left in a pocket"
    description: "A forgotten item like a pen, lighter, or small toy can bang around inside the drum itself during spin — the simplest and most common cause to rule out first."
safeChecks:
  - "Check pockets and the drum thoroughly for any forgotten small items before assuming a mechanical fault."
  - "On front-loaders, run your fingers carefully around the full fold of the door boot gasket (unplugged) to check for anything trapped."
  - "Unplug the machine and gently rock the tub by hand (where accessible) to check for looseness in the counterweight or drum support."
  - "Note whether the bang happens at all spin speeds or only once the machine reaches higher RPM."
steps:
  - title: "Empty and check the drum thoroughly"
    description: "Remove all laundry and run a hand around the inside of the drum and, on front-loaders, the door boot fold for anything trapped or forgotten."
  - title: "Run an empty test cycle"
    description: "With the drum confirmed empty, run a spin-only cycle and listen — if the bang continues with nothing inside, it points toward a mechanical cause rather than a forgotten object."
  - title: "Inspect visible counterweight bolts"
    description: "On many top-loaders, the counterweight is accessible by removing the cabinet top or rear panel — check that mounting bolts are tight and the block itself isn't cracked."
  - title: "Check for shaking alongside the banging"
    description: "If the machine also shakes or walks, work through our shaking/vibration guide too, since leveling and load balance can compound a banging noise from a separate cause."
dontDo:
  - "Don't run the machine repeatedly with a loose counterweight or drum support — continued operation can worsen the damage and, in rare cases, cause a component to detach further."
  - "Don't ignore a banging noise that continues on an empty, confirmed-clear drum — this points to something mechanical that needs attention."
  - "Don't attempt to access or re-bolt a counterweight without unplugging the machine first."
whenToCallPro:
  - "The bang continues on an empty drum with nothing trapped in the door boot fold."
  - "You find a loose or cracked counterweight but aren't comfortable accessing or securing it yourself."
  - "You suspect a detached or damaged drum support/spider arm — this typically requires significant disassembly."
  - "The banging is getting louder or more frequent over successive loads."
faqs:
  - q: "How is banging different from the shaking my washer already does sometimes?"
    a: "Shaking or 'walking' is a rhythmic wobble from imbalance, an unlevel floor, or worn suspension — the whole machine moves. Banging is a distinct, usually louder impact sound, often once per rotation, suggesting something is physically striking part of the machine rather than the whole unit swaying."
  - q: "Could it just be something in my pocket?"
    a: "Yes, and it's worth ruling out first — pens, coins, and small toys are a surprisingly common and easy-to-fix cause of a banging sound during spin."
  - q: "Is a loose counterweight dangerous to keep using?"
    a: "It's not an immediate safety hazard in most cases, but continuing to run the machine can loosen it further or crack it more, turning a simple re-tightening into a full part replacement — worth addressing promptly."
