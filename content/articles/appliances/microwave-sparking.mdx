quickAnswer: "Sparking almost always comes from metal (even a thin foil edge or metallic trim on a plate) or a damaged/greasy waveguide cover inside the cavity. Stop the microwave immediately and inspect before running it again."
symptoms:
  - "Visible sparks or flashes of light inside the cavity during operation"
  - "A crackling or popping sound while heating"
  - "Small burn marks appearing on the interior walls"
  - "A burning smell during or after use"
causes:
  - title: "Metal inside the microwave"
    description: "Foil, metal-trimmed dishes, twist ties, or even a fork left in food will reflect microwaves and cause visible arcing."
  - title: "Damaged or greasy waveguide cover"
    description: "The small rectangular cover (usually on a side or top wall) protects the waveguide from food splatter. Grease buildup or a burnt hole in this cover is one of the most common sparking causes."
  - title: "Chipped or cracked interior enamel"
    description: "A metal support rack or tray edge scraping the cavity wall can chip the protective coating, exposing metal underneath that then arcs."
  - title: "Empty or near-empty operation"
    description: "Running a microwave with little or no food/liquid inside gives the energy nothing to absorb, which can damage the magnetron and cause arcing over repeated occurrences."
safeChecks:
  - "Stop the microwave and unplug it as soon as you notice sparking — don't wait for the cycle to finish."
  - "Open the door and visually check for any metal object, twist tie, or metallic-trimmed dish."
  - "Inspect the waveguide cover for scorch marks, holes, or heavy grease buildup."
  - "Look at the interior walls for chips in the enamel coating that expose bare metal."
steps:
  - title: "Stop and unplug immediately"
    description: "Sparking is not something to wait out — stop the microwave and disconnect power right away."
  - title: "Remove the cause"
    description: "Take out any metal item, foil, or dish causing the arcing."
  - title: "Clean the waveguide cover"
    description: "If the cover is greasy but intact, remove it per your manual's instructions and wash it, letting it dry fully before reinstalling."
  - title: "Replace a damaged waveguide cover"
    description: "If the cover is burnt, cracked, or has a hole, replace it with the exact manufacturer part before using the microwave again — this part is inexpensive and widely available."
  - title: "Test with a mug of water"
    description: "Once the cause is addressed, test briefly with just a mug of water (no other items) and watch closely for any further sparking."
dontDo:
  - "Don't continue running the microwave 'to see if it stops' — repeated arcing can damage the magnetron and, in rare cases, start a fire."
  - "Don't use any metal cookware, twist ties, or foil unless your manual explicitly says a specific item is microwave-safe."
  - "Don't run the microwave empty to test it — always include a microwave-safe liquid or food."
whenToCallPro:
  - "Sparking continues even after removing metal and cleaning/replacing the waveguide cover."
  - "You see a chip in the interior enamel exposing bare metal — this typically requires professional repair or replacement of the unit."
  - "There's a burning smell that persists after the microwave has cooled and been inspected."
  - "The unit is old and this isn't the first time it has sparked."
faqs:
  - q: "Is it safe to keep using a microwave after it sparks once?"
    a: "Only after you've identified and removed the cause. If you can't find an obvious reason (metal, damaged waveguide cover), don't use it again until it's been checked."
  - q: "Can a paper towel or plate cause sparking?"
    a: "Regular paper and ceramic without metallic trim are safe. Sparking from these is unlikely — check for metallic paint, staples, or trim you might have missed."
  - q: "How do I know if my waveguide cover needs replacing?"
    a: "If it looks scorched, has a hole burned through it, or is heavily saturated with grease that won't clean off, replace it rather than continuing to use it."

safetyWarning: "Never operate a microwave with visible damage to the interior, door seal, or waveguide cover. If sparking is severe, accompanied by smoke, or the unit doesn't power off normally, unplug it at the wall (don't just close the door) and stop using it permanently until inspected by a technician."
