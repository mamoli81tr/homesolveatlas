A locked door after the cycle ends actually tells you something useful by elimination. If the display shows the cycle complete and the drum has fully stopped, that already rules out the mid-cycle safety interlock — the mechanism that specifically stops the door opening while the drum is moving. That's a completely different system from whatever is holding the lock now. What's left is a timed release delay, a drain-confirmation check, or a genuinely stuck latch — all things that hold the door *after* a cycle, not the spin-safety interlock that matters *during* one.

The most common mistake at this point is treating a normal few-minute delay as a fault and immediately reaching for tools or force. Most locked-door calls resolve on their own within the wait time described above, well before anything is actually broken.
