quickAnswer: "This usually isn't a machine fault at all — plastic has much lower thermal mass than glass or ceramic, so it doesn't hold heat through the drying stage the way heavier items do, and condensation-based drying (which many machines rely on, with or without a heating element) is far less effective on plastic as a result. Rinse aid and loading position make a real, noticeable difference."
symptoms:
  - "Plastic containers and lids come out visibly wet or with water droplets"
  - "Glass, ceramic, and metal items from the same load are fully dry"
  - "The problem is consistent across most or all loads, not occasional"
  - "Drying seems worse on eco or low-heat cycle settings specifically"
causes:
  - title: "Plastic's low thermal mass"
    description: "Glass and ceramic retain heat from the final hot rinse long enough for that heat to drive off remaining moisture; lightweight plastic cools too quickly for the same effect, which is a basic physical property rather than a fault."
  - title: "Condensation-drying design"
    description: "Many dishwashers dry primarily through condensation (moisture drawn to cooler surfaces) rather than active heated air — this works reasonably well on heavy items but poorly on plastic regardless of machine condition."
  - title: "Insufficient rinse aid"
    description: "Rinse aid causes water to sheet off surfaces rather than form droplets, which matters even more for plastic specifically since it doesn't have residual heat to help evaporate droplets on its own."
  - title: "Loading position"
    description: "Plastic items angled flat or nested so water pools in a well or indentation will stay wetter than items angled to let water run off."
safeChecks:
  - "Check your rinse aid dispenser and refill it if low or empty."
  - "Check how plastic items are angled in the rack — items with concave surfaces should be tilted so water can run off rather than pool."
  - "Check whether you're using an eco or low-heat cycle, which typically dries less effectively on all items, plastic especially."
  - "Note whether this is consistent across most loads (expected) versus a sudden new problem (worth investigating further)."
steps:
  - title: "Fill the rinse aid dispenser"
    description: "This is the single most effective, low-effort improvement for plastic drying specifically."
  - title: "Angle plastic items to drain"
    description: "Load cups, containers, and lids at an angle rather than flat, so water runs off instead of pooling in the base."
  - title: "Use a heated-dry cycle setting if available"
    description: "If your machine offers a heated dry option separate from the default eco setting, use it for loads with a lot of plastic."
  - title: "Towel-dry stubborn items as a practical workaround"
    description: "For particularly troublesome pieces (deep, flat-bottomed containers especially), a quick manual towel-dry remains a reasonable practical step even with everything else optimized."
dontDo:
  - "Don't assume this means your dishwasher is broken if glass and ceramic are drying normally — that's a strong sign the machine itself is working fine."
  - "Don't skip rinse aid thinking it only affects spotting — it has a real, direct effect on drying performance too."
  - "Don't load plastic items flat with a concave surface facing up, which just creates a small pool that won't evaporate on its own."
whenToCallPro:
  - "Glass and ceramic items have also started coming out wet, which points to an actual drying-system fault rather than plastic's inherent properties."
  - "You've tried rinse aid, loading position, and a heated-dry setting with no improvement at all."
faqs:
  - q: "Is my dishwasher's heating element broken if plastic doesn't dry?"
    a: "Unlikely, especially if glass and ceramic items dry normally — that's a good sign the heating element and overall drying system are working. Plastic drying poorly is largely an inherent property of the material, not a fault."
  - q: "Does rinse aid really make a noticeable difference on plastic?"
    a: "Yes — since plastic can't rely on residual heat the way glass does, helping water sheet off cleanly via rinse aid matters proportionally more for plastic items than for heavier ones."
  - q: "Should I just avoid putting plastic in the dishwasher?"
    a: "Not necessary — with rinse aid topped up, plastic angled to drain, and a heated-dry setting when available, most households get acceptably dry results without needing to hand-wash plastic separately."

Because glass and plates are coming out dry, this rules out an actual heating or drying-system fault — the real explanation is a basic physical difference in how plastic holds heat compared to heavier materials.

| What you notice | Most likely cause |
|---|---|

| Water pooled in the base of containers | Loading position, not angled to drain |
| Everything seems wetter than it used to | Check rinse aid level first |
| Glass/ceramic also started coming out wet | A genuine drying-system issue — treat differently |
