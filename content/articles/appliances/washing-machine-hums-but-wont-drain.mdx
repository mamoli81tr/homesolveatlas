quickAnswer: "A hum during the drain stage means the pump motor is receiving power and trying to spin — the electrical side is fine. The water isn't leaving because something mechanical is stopping it: most often a foreign object jammed in the pump impeller, less often a seized pump bearing."
symptoms:
  - "A steady or pulsing hum during what should be the drain stage"
  - "Water level in the drum doesn't drop while the hum is present"
  - "The hum stops on its own after a few seconds (thermal cutout) rather than continuing indefinitely"
  - "No burning smell, just a mechanical-sounding hum"
causes:
  - title: "Foreign object jammed in the pump impeller"
    description: "Coins, buttons, bra underwires, and small toys are the classic culprits — they lodge in the pump's impeller blades and physically block it from spinning even though the motor is trying."
  - title: "Impeller disconnected or broken"
    description: "On some models the plastic impeller can crack or slip off its shaft with age, so the motor spins freely (or hums against resistance) without actually moving water."
  - title: "Seized or worn pump bearing"
    description: "Years of use can wear out the bearing the pump motor spins on, causing it to strain and hum under load instead of turning smoothly."
  - title: "Pump motor nearing failure"
    description: "If nothing is jammed and the bearing is fine, the pump motor itself may be weak or failing, humming as it tries and fails to reach full speed."
safeChecks:
  - "Unplug the washer before doing anything else — never reach toward a pump while the machine is powered."
  - "Check the pump filter (see our not-draining guide) for anything visibly jammed, since this is the same access point used to reach the impeller on many models."
  - "Listen for whether the hum is continuous or pulses — a continuous hum with no cutoff can mean a stalled motor under constant load."
  - "Note whether this started right after washing an item likely to have loose hardware (bra, jeans with a stud, a child's small toy)."
steps:
  - title: "Unplug the machine first"
    description: "Disconnect power completely before opening the filter or pump access panel — the pump can still be live even mid-cycle."
  - title: "Open the pump filter and inspect the impeller"
    description: "Behind the small round filter cap (see our not-draining guide for the exact access point), you can often see or reach the impeller directly. Remove any visible foreign object by hand."
  - title: "Spin the impeller by hand"
    description: "With power off, try turning the impeller with your fingers or a small tool. It should move smoothly; heavy resistance or grinding suggests a worn bearing rather than a simple jam."
  - title: "Reassemble and test"
    description: "Once clear, close the filter, restore power, and run a drain-only or spin cycle to confirm the hum is gone and water clears normally."
dontDo:
  - "Don't run repeated cycles hoping the hum will resolve itself — a jammed impeller won't clear on its own and continued running can burn out the motor."
  - "Don't reach into the pump housing with the machine plugged in, even if the drum looks empty."
  - "Don't force a seized impeller with excessive strength — if it won't turn at all by hand, that points to a bearing or motor problem needing a technician rather than a simple clog."
whenToCallPro:
  - "The impeller won't turn by hand even after clearing visible debris."
  - "The hum continues with no change after removing a jam and reassembling."
  - "You hear grinding along with the hum, suggesting a worn bearing."
  - "The pump motor needs replacing — this typically involves accessing wiring connectors best handled by a technician."
faqs:
  - q: "Does humming mean the pump is broken?"
    a: "Not necessarily — a hum most often means the motor is fine electrically but is mechanically blocked, usually by something jammed in the impeller. A worn bearing or failing motor is possible but less common."
  - q: "Is it safe to keep using the washer while it hums during drain?"
    a: "Stop and investigate instead. Running a motor that's straining against a jam repeatedly can overheat and shorten its life, turning an easy fix into a full pump replacement."
  - q: "How is this different from a washer that's just not draining at all?"
    a: "A washer that's silent during a failed drain often has a simple blockage upstream (filter, hose) or no power reaching the pump. A hum specifically tells you the pump motor itself is engaged and struggling, which points more directly at the impeller or bearing."
sources:
