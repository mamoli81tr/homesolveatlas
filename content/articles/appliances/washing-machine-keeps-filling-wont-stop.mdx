Once the immediate risk is handled — supply off, machine unplugged — one test tells you which half of the system to focus on:

**Briefly restore the water supply with the machine still unplugged.**

- Water flows into the machine with zero power connected → the inlet valve itself is stuck open, mechanically, independent of any signal from the control board. It needs replacing; there's nothing to test further upstream.
- No water flows with the machine unplugged → the valve is behaving normally and only opens when *told* to. That shifts the likely cause to the sensing side — the pressure switch or its air tube not telling the control board when to send the "stop" command in the first place.

This single test is worth doing before assuming which part has failed, since a stuck valve and a failed sensor are different repairs, and guessing wrong means buying the wrong part.
