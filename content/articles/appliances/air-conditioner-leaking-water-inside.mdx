quickAnswer: "Water leaking inside almost always means condensate isn't draining to the outside as designed — usually because the unit isn't tilted slightly downward toward the outdoors, or the internal drain pan or drain hole is clogged with dust and debris."
symptoms:
  - "Water dripping from the front or bottom of the unit into the room"
  - "A puddle forming on the windowsill or floor beneath the unit"
  - "Leaking is worse on very humid days"
  - "Leaking started after the unit was recently installed or moved"
causes:
  - title: "Unit not tilted correctly (window units)"
    description: "Window AC units are designed to sit tilted very slightly toward the outside (usually about a quarter-inch to half-inch lower on the outdoor side) so condensate naturally drains outward instead of pooling inside."
  - title: "Clogged internal drain hole or pan"
    description: "Dust and debris can block the small drain opening that lets excess condensate escape, causing it to overflow inside instead."
  - title: "Clogged condensate drain line (portable units)"
    description: "Portable ACs route condensate to an internal tank or an external hose — a kinked hose or a full/clogged internal tank causes backup and indoor leaking."
  - title: "Dirty air filter causing coil icing"
    description: "A clogged filter can cause the coil to ice over; when that ice then melts faster than the drain can handle, it can overwhelm the drain path and leak inside."
safeChecks:
  - "Check whether the unit is tilted toward the outside as intended — a bubble level on top of the unit will show this clearly."
  - "For portable units, check whether the internal tank is full or the drain hose is kinked."
  - "Check the air filter's condition, since a clogged filter contributing to coil icing can indirectly cause overflow."
steps:
  - title: "Correct the unit's tilt (window units)"
    description: "With help, carefully adjust the unit so the outdoor side sits slightly lower than the indoor side — many units have small adjustable feet or shims for exactly this purpose."
  - title: "Clear the drain hole and pan"
    description: "With the unit off and, if possible, removed from the window per your manual's instructions, clear any visible debris from the internal drain opening and pan."
  - title: "Check the portable unit's tank or hose"
    description: "Empty a full internal tank, or straighten any kink in the external drain hose so gravity can do its job."
  - title: "Clean or replace the filter"
    description: "A clean filter reduces the chance of coil icing that can overwhelm the drain system when it melts."
dontDo:
  - "Don't tilt a window unit the wrong direction (higher on the outdoor side) — this guarantees indoor leaking regardless of anything else."
  - "Don't ignore a small indoor drip hoping it will stop — it usually means the tilt or drain needs correcting, not that it will resolve itself."
  - "Don't force a stuck internal tank or hose fitting on a portable unit — check your model's manual for the correct release mechanism."
whenToCallPro:
  - "The tilt is correct, the drain is clear, and the unit still leaks inside."
  - "You notice water leaking specifically when the compressor is running rather than just condensation, which could indicate a refrigerant issue."
  - "The leak is from a larger split-system indoor unit rather than a window or portable unit — this typically involves a condensate pump worth having inspected."
faqs:
  - q: "How much should a window AC be tilted?"
    a: "A slight tilt of about a quarter to half an inch lower on the outdoor side is typical — enough for gravity to carry condensate outward, without being so steep that the unit feels unstable in the window frame."
  - q: "Is some condensation normal?"
    a: "Yes, a small amount of moisture is a normal byproduct of cooling humid air. The issue is specifically water accumulating and dripping inside the room rather than draining outside as designed."
  - q: "Do portable ACs need to be drained manually?"
    a: "Many portable units collect some condensate in an internal tank that needs periodic emptying unless connected to a continuous external drain hose — check your specific model's manual for its drainage setup."
