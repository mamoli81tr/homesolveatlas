quickAnswer: "Most drainage failures come from a clogged drain filter or pump, a kinked or crushed drain hose, or a blocked standpipe — in that order of likelihood. Clearing the filter fixes the majority of cases in under 20 minutes."
symptoms:
  - "Cycle ends with several inches of water still sitting in the drum"
  - "Machine hums or vibrates during the drain stage but water level doesn't drop"
  - "Error code related to drainage appears on digital displays (e.g. LE, 5d, OE)"
  - "Clothes come out of the wash noticeably wetter than usual"
causes:
  - title: "Clogged drain filter"
    description: "Lint, coins, hair ties, and small fabric fibers collect in the pump filter over time until water can no longer pass through it."
  - title: "Kinked or crushed drain hose"
    description: "If the machine was recently moved or pushed against a wall, the flexible drain hose can bend sharply enough to block flow."
  - title: "Blocked standpipe or drain line"
    description: "The wall standpipe the hose empties into can clog with lint and detergent residue, especially in older plumbing."
  - title: "Failed drain pump"
    description: "The pump motor or its impeller can wear out or jam on a foreign object, so it can't push water out even with no blockage."
  - title: "Overloaded or unbalanced load"
    description: "An extremely heavy or lopsided load can trip a safety sensor that pauses the drain cycle before it completes."
safeChecks:
  - "Unplug the machine (or switch off at the wall) before opening any access panel."
  - "Check the drain hose at the back for visible kinks, and straighten it if the machine has been moved."
  - "Look at the small drain filter door near the bottom front of the machine and see if it's due for cleaning."
  - "Run a short spin-only cycle to see whether the pump makes its normal whirring sound or a strained/silent one."
steps:
  - title: "Cut power and place towels down"
    description: "Unplug the washer. Water will spill when you open the filter, so lay towels or a shallow tray in front of the access panel."
  - title: "Open the drain filter and empty it"
    description: "Most front-loaders have a small round filter cap behind a flap near the bottom front. Turn it slowly counter-clockwise — water will drain out — then pull it fully and remove lint, coins, or debris."
  - title: "Check the drain hose"
    description: "Pull the machine out slightly and inspect the corrugated drain hose for kinks, tight bends, or a crushed section. Straighten it and re-route it if needed."
  - title: "Clear the standpipe if the hose and filter are clean"
    description: "If water still won't drain, the wall standpipe may be blocked. A wet/dry vacuum on the standpipe opening can pull out a soft clog."
  - title: "Run a drain or spin cycle to confirm"
    description: "Reassemble the filter, plug the machine back in, and run a drain-only or spin cycle to confirm water clears completely."
dontDo:
  - "Don't open the drain filter without unplugging the machine and laying down towels — it releases standing water."
  - "Don't force the filter cap if it won't turn; forcing it can crack the plastic housing."
  - "Don't run repeated full wash cycles hoping it clears itself — this risks overflow and motor strain."
  - "Don't use chemical drain cleaners inside the machine — they're not designed for this and can damage seals."
whenToCallPro:
  - "Water still won't drain after the filter and hose are confirmed clear — this points to a failed pump."
  - "You hear grinding, buzzing, or no sound at all from the pump during the drain stage."
  - "There's a burning smell, which can indicate a failing pump motor and is a fire-safety concern."
  - "The machine is leaking from underneath in addition to not draining."
faqs:
  - q: "Why does my washing machine fill but not drain?"
    a: "This usually means the pump is trying to push water out but can't, most often because the filter or hose is blocked. If the pump itself has failed, you may hear no drain noise at all."
  - q: "Is it safe to run the washer again if it didn't drain?"
    a: "Avoid starting a new wash cycle until the standing water is dealt with — it can overflow or add strain to an already struggling pump."
  - q: "How often should I clean the drain filter?"
    a: "Every 1–3 months for average use, or more often if you regularly wash pet bedding, sandy items, or items with loose fibers."
sources:
  - "Check your washing machine's owner manual for the exact filter location — it varies by brand and model."
safetyWarning: "Always disconnect power before opening any access panel, and never touch electrical components with wet hands. If you smell burning or see scorch marks near the motor, stop and call a licensed appliance technician."

Front-loading and high-efficiency top-loading washers share a similar drain path: pump filter → drain hose → household drain line. Working through the checklist in that order finds the blockage fastest, since the filter is both the easiest to check and the most common culprit.
