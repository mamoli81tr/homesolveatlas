quickAnswer: "If the pump audibly runs at normal speed (not straining or humming) but water never leaves, the pump itself is working — the problem is downstream: a kinked or blocked drain hose, a clogged standpipe, or an impeller that's spinning but not actually gripping the water due to a cracked or loose fit."
symptoms:
  - "A normal-sounding pump running noise during the drain stage"
  - "No straining, humming, or grinding — the pump sounds healthy"
  - "Water level in the drum stays the same the whole time the pump runs"
  - "The pump eventually times out and the machine shows a drain-related fault"
causes:
  - title: "Kinked or crushed drain hose"
    description: "If the hose bends sharply behind the machine, the pump can run perfectly and still move no water, since the path out is physically blocked."
  - title: "Blocked standpipe or household drain"
    description: "With the pump and hose both confirmed clear, the blockage may be further downstream in the wall standpipe or household plumbing the washer empties into."
  - title: "Impeller spinning without gripping water"
    description: "A cracked impeller, or one that has slipped loose on its drive shaft, can spin freely without pushing water — sounding like a normally running pump while doing no actual work."
  - title: "Drain hose routed too high"
    description: "Most machines need the drain hose's high point kept within a specific height range (commonly around 3–4 feet); a hose routed too high can prevent the pump from pushing water over that height even at full power."
safeChecks:
  - "Unplug the washer before inspecting the hose or filter."
  - "Pull the machine out and check the full length of the drain hose for kinks or a tight bend."
  - "Check the height the drain hose reaches before it enters the standpipe or drain — compare against your manual's recommended maximum."
  - "Run a nearby sink and see if it drains slowly too, which would point to a shared household blockage rather than a washer-specific one."
steps:
  - title: "Confirm the pump sounds healthy"
    description: "Listen carefully — a smooth, consistent running sound (not humming or straining) suggests the motor and impeller are engaging normally, pointing you downstream."
  - title: "Straighten and check the drain hose"
    description: "Inspect the full hose run for kinks, and confirm it isn't looped higher than your model's maximum recommended height."
  - title: "Clear the standpipe"
    description: "If the hose is clear, use a wet/dry vacuum at the standpipe opening to pull out any soft clog further down the line."
  - title: "Inspect the impeller for a loose or cracked fit"
    description: "With power off, open the pump filter access point and check whether the impeller is firmly seated on its shaft and free of cracks — a loose or damaged impeller needs replacing rather than clearing."
dontDo:
  - "Don't assume the pump has failed just because water isn't draining — a healthy-sounding pump usually means the fault is elsewhere."
  - "Don't route a drain hose higher than your machine's manual specifies, even temporarily — it can prevent draining regardless of pump condition."
  - "Don't force or bend a kinked hose back into a tight space without addressing why it kinked in the first place (usually the machine sitting too close to a wall)."
whenToCallPro:
  - "The hose, standpipe, and impeller all check out fine but water still won't drain."
  - "You find a cracked or loose impeller, which typically needs a replacement part and proper reseating."
  - "You suspect a blockage inside your home's plumbing beyond the washer's own drain hose."
faqs:
  - q: "If the pump sounds fine, can it still be the problem?"
    a: "Yes, in one specific case: if the impeller has cracked or come loose on its shaft, the motor can sound completely normal while doing no actual pumping work. This is less common than a hose or standpipe blockage but worth checking if everything downstream is confirmed clear."
  - q: "How high can a drain hose be routed?"
    a: "Check your specific model's manual, but many washers specify a maximum drain height around 3–4 feet (roughly 96 cm) above the floor — routing it higher can prevent the pump from clearing water even when everything else works."
  - q: "Is this different from a washer that hums during drain?"
    a: "Yes — humming suggests the pump is straining against a mechanical block (like a jammed impeller). A normal running sound with no drainage points away from the pump itself and toward the hose or plumbing downstream."
