quickAnswer: "A pod found whole or partly dissolved after a cycle usually means one of three things: the dispenser door never opened, the water reaching it wasn't hot enough to fully break down the outer film, or something blocked the spray arm from hitting the dispenser directly. Checking in that order finds the cause quickly."

  - "The problem happens more with cold or eco/quick cycles than normal ones"

  - title: "Something blocks the spray arm from reaching the dispenser"
    description: "If a tall item or a dish is loaded in front of the detergent dispenser, the spray arm may not hit it directly, leaving the pod without enough direct water contact to dissolve."
  - title: "Pod stuck against the rack or a utensil"
    description: "If the door does open but the pod tumbles against something and stays lodged against the door itself, it may not get pushed clear into the wash stream."

Whether the dispenser door opened or not is the fastest way to split this into two different problems — a still-closed door points to a specific mechanical fault (see our detergent door guide), while an open door with an undissolved pod points to water temperature or spray access instead.

| What you notice | Most likely cause |
|---|---|
| Dispenser door is still closed | Door mechanism fault — see our detergent door guide |
| Door is open, pod is loose in the tub | Water temperature or spray-arm access |
| Pod is stuck against the door itself | Pod tumbled against an obstruction |
| Only happens on cold/eco cycles | Insufficient heat to dissolve the film |
