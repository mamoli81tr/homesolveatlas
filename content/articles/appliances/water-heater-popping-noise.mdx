quickAnswer: "Popping, cracking, or rumbling sounds almost always come from mineral sediment that has settled at the bottom of the tank — trapped water boils underneath it and escapes in small bursts, making the noise you hear."
symptoms:
  - "Popping or crackling sounds during the heating cycle"
  - "Rumbling noise that grows louder over months"
  - "Reduced hot water capacity compared to when the unit was new"
  - "Longer time needed to reach full hot water temperature"
causes:
  - title: "Sediment buildup on the tank bottom"
    description: "Minerals from your water supply settle at the bottom of the tank over time, especially in areas with hard water. The burner heats water trapped under this layer, which pops as steam bubbles escape."
  - title: "Never having been flushed"
    description: "Tanks that have gone years without a maintenance flush accumulate a thicker sediment layer, which makes the popping more pronounced."
  - title: "High water hardness"
    description: "Homes on well water or with notably hard municipal water build sediment faster and may need more frequent flushing than the typical annual schedule."
steps:
  - title: "Turn off power or gas to the unit"
    description: "For electric units, switch off the breaker. For gas units, turn the control to 'pilot' or off per the manufacturer's instructions before starting any maintenance."
  - title: "Let the water cool"
    description: "Give the tank a few hours to cool if it's been recently used, to avoid scalding during the flush."
  - title: "Connect a hose to the drain valve"
    description: "Attach a garden hose to the drain valve near the bottom of the tank and run the other end to a floor drain or outside."
  - title: "Flush the tank"
    description: "Close the cold water supply valve, open the drain valve and a hot water tap elsewhere in the house to let air in, and let the tank drain fully, flushing until the water runs clear."
  - title: "Refill and restore power"
    description: "Close the drain valve, reopen the cold water supply, let the tank refill completely (check a hot tap runs steadily with no air sputtering), then restore power or gas."
safeChecks:
  - "Confirm which type of water heater you have (gas or electric) before touching any controls — the shutdown procedure differs."
  - "Check the manufacturer's manual for your model's specific flush and shutoff instructions."
  - "Inspect the drain valve area for any existing corrosion or leaks before connecting a hose."
dontDo:
  - "Don't skip turning off power or gas before draining — running a heating element or burner without water can destroy it."
  - "Don't flush a tank you suspect has active leaks or heavy corrosion without professional guidance first."
  - "Don't ignore steadily worsening popping noises — prolonged sediment buildup shortens the tank's lifespan and can lead to premature failure."

  - "You notice rust-colored water, a persistent gas smell, or any visible leaking around the tank base."
  - "Popping continues after a full flush, which can point to a failing heating element (electric) or burner issue (gas)."
faqs:
  - q: "Is a popping water heater dangerous?"
    a: "Not immediately, but ignored sediment buildup reduces efficiency and can shorten the tank's life. A gas smell or visible leak, on the other hand, is a safety issue that needs immediate professional attention."
  - q: "How often should I flush my water heater?"
    a: "Most manufacturers recommend once a year, or twice a year if you have hard water."
  - q: "Will flushing the tank stop the noise completely?"
    a: "It resolves noise caused by sediment in most cases. If popping continues after a thorough flush, a technician should check the heating element or burner assembly."
