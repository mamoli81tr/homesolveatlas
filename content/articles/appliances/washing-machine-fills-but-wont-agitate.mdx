quickAnswer: "A washer that fills but never agitates has a different fault than one that won't spin — agitation and spin use overlapping but distinct mechanisms. On top-loaders, worn agitator dogs (small ratcheting cams inside the agitator) are the single most common cause; on both top- and front-loaders, a slipped or broken drive belt is the next most likely."
symptoms:
  - "Water fills to the correct level, then nothing happens — no back-and-forth or tumbling motion"
  - "The machine may sit silently, or the motor may hum without moving the drum/agitator"
  - "The cycle may eventually proceed to drain and spin without ever having washed the clothes"
  - "Clothes come out clean-smelling only from the detergent, not actually agitated"
causes:
  - title: "Worn agitator dogs (top-load machines)"
    description: "Inside a top-load agitator are small plastic ratcheting cams called dogs, which grip during one direction of motion to twist the clothes. When they wear down, the agitator can spin freely without moving the basket or clothes."
  - title: "Slipped or broken drive belt"
    description: "The belt connecting the motor to the transmission (which drives both agitation and spin on many machines) can slip, stretch, or break, cutting off agitation specifically if it disconnects at the wrong point in its travel."
  - title: "Motor coupling failure"
    description: "On direct-drive machines, a worn motor coupling can fail in a way that affects agitation while leaving other functions partially working, or vice versa."
  - title: "Control board not sending the agitate signal"
    description: "Less commonly, a control board fault can mean the machine simply never commands agitation to start, even though the fill and later stages proceed normally."
safeChecks:
  - "Unplug the machine before opening the agitator or accessing the belt."
  - "On a top-loader, remove the agitator cap and try turning the agitator by hand in both directions — it should grip and resist in one direction and turn more freely in the other. If it turns freely both ways, the dogs are likely worn."
  - "Listen closely during the wash stage for a motor sound with no visible movement, which points toward a belt or coupling issue rather than a control signal problem."
  - "Check whether spin works normally at the end of the cycle — if spin works but agitate doesn't, that helps narrow down which shared components are and aren't at fault."
steps:
  - title: "Test the agitator by hand (top-load)"
    description: "With power off, remove the agitator's softener cap and center bolt cover, then try rotating the agitator both directions to check if the ratcheting dogs are gripping as expected."
  - title: "Inspect the drive belt"
    description: "Access the belt (location varies by model — often behind a rear or bottom panel) and check for visible slack, cracking, or a belt that's slipped off its pulley."
  - title: "Replace worn agitator dogs or a bad belt"
    description: "Both are defined, commonly available parts. Agitator dog kits are usually straightforward; belt replacement involves more disassembly and correct tensioning."
  - title: "Test a full cycle"
    description: "After repairs, run a normal cycle and confirm agitation, drain, and spin all work through a complete cycle."
dontDo:
  - "Don't force the agitator by hand with significant strength — if it's seized rather than free-spinning, that's a different problem needing inspection first."
  - "Don't skip checking whether spin works before assuming the whole transmission has failed — isolating agitate-only failure narrows the likely cause significantly."
  - "Don't run the machine repeatedly on the same load hoping agitation will start — extended motor operation against a fault can cause further damage."
whenToCallPro:
  - "The agitator dogs are worn and you're not comfortable replacing the kit yourself."
  - "The drive belt is broken or slipping and needs replacement, which often requires machine disassembly."
  - "You suspect a motor coupling or control board fault rather than the belt or agitator dogs."
  - "Spin also fails alongside agitation, suggesting a more central transmission or motor problem."
faqs:
  - q: "How do I know if it's the agitator dogs specifically?"
    a: "The clearest sign is the agitator spinning freely in both directions by hand with no resistance or ratcheting feel — healthy dogs grip firmly in one direction while allowing free movement in the other."
  - q: "Is this the same fault as a washer that won't spin?"
    a: "Not necessarily — agitation and spin are different stages that can share some components (like the belt) but fail independently. A machine can agitate fine and fail to spin, or vice versa, depending on exactly what's worn or broken."
  - q: "Are agitator dog kits expensive to replace?"
    a: "They're generally one of the less expensive washer parts and are designed as a straightforward wear item, though actual difficulty and cost can vary by model — check parts availability for your specific machine."
