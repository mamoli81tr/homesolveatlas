quickAnswer: "A grinding or scraping sound during spin is a different symptom from shaking or banging — it points to metal-on-metal or plastic-on-metal wear, most often a worn drum bearing, a worn motor pulley, or a small object (coin, wire, button) trapped between the inner drum and outer tub."
symptoms:
  - "A grinding, scraping, or rumbling sound specifically during spin"
  - "The noise may get louder as spin speed increases"
  - "The machine may still spin at a normal or near-normal speed despite the noise"
  - "No significant shaking or walking — the noise is the primary symptom, not movement"
causes:
  - title: "Worn drum bearing"
    description: "The bearing that supports the inner drum's rotation wears out over years of use, producing an increasingly loud grinding or rumbling sound as it deteriorates — often worse at higher spin speeds."
  - title: "Small object trapped between the drum and tub"
    description: "Coins, underwire, buttons, or other small hard items can fall through drum perforations and get caught between the inner drum and outer tub, grinding with every rotation."
  - title: "Worn motor or drive pulley"
    description: "The pulley the drive belt rides on can wear unevenly, producing a grinding sound as the belt no longer sits and turns smoothly."
  - title: "Worn spider arm or drum support"
    description: "On some front-loaders, the spider (the structural support connecting the drum to the drive shaft) can corrode or crack, allowing metal-on-metal contact that grinds during rotation."
safeChecks:
  - "Unplug the machine before any closer inspection."
  - "Spin the empty drum by hand (unplugged) and listen for grinding at low, manual speed — this can help confirm a bearing or pulley issue independent of motor operation."
  - "Check inside the drum and around the door boot/gasket for any small object that might have fallen through the drum's holes."
  - "Note whether the sound changes with load size — a trapped object often sounds different or more erratic than a worn bearing, which tends to be more constant."
steps:
  - title: "Check for a trapped object first"
    description: "This is the easiest cause to rule out — inspect the drum thoroughly and, on front-loaders, check around and behind the door boot fold for anything stuck in the gap."
  - title: "Manually spin the empty drum"
    description: "With power off and the machine empty, turn the drum by hand and listen and feel for grinding or roughness, which points toward the bearing or pulley rather than something loose."
  - title: "Inspect the drive pulley and belt area if accessible"
    description: "Check for visible wear, uneven surfaces, or debris on the pulley the belt rides on."
  - title: "Have the bearing or spider assessed if the sound persists"
    description: "A worn drum bearing or spider arm typically requires disassembling the machine to access and replace, and is a job best handled by a technician given the labor involved."
dontDo:
  - "Don't ignore a new grinding noise hoping it will go away — bearing wear typically worsens over time and can eventually damage the drum or tub further."
  - "Don't run heavy loads on a machine grinding loudly, which adds more stress to an already-worn bearing or pulley."
  - "Don't attempt to disassemble the drum or tub assembly yourself unless you're experienced — this is a more involved repair than most appliance DIY tasks."
whenToCallPro:
  - "You've ruled out a trapped object and the grinding continues, especially when manually spinning the empty drum."
  - "The noise is worsening over successive loads."
  - "You suspect a worn drum bearing or spider arm, both of which typically require significant disassembly to replace."
  - "The repair cost estimate approaches a significant fraction of a new machine's price — worth weighing repair against replacement, especially on an older unit."
faqs:
  - q: "Is grinding different from the shaking/banging my washer sometimes does?"
    a: "Yes — shaking and banging are about the machine's balance and movement (unbalanced loads, leveling, suspension). Grinding is a wear-related sound from components rubbing or scraping, and can happen even in a level, well-balanced machine."
  - q: "How much does a drum bearing replacement typically involve?"
    a: "It varies by model, but it's generally one of the more labor-intensive washer repairs since it usually requires substantial disassembly to access — get a repair quote to compare against your machine's age and value."
  - q: "Can a small trapped object really cause a loud grinding sound?"
    a: "Yes — even a small coin or piece of hardware caught between the drum and tub can produce a surprisingly loud grinding or clunking sound with every rotation, and it's worth thoroughly checking for before assuming a bigger mechanical fault."
