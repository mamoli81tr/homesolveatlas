quickAnswer: "If the wash stage completes normally but the machine consistently stalls during rinse, the cause is usually specific to that stage: a water inlet valve too weak to refill for rinse, excess suds confusing the water-level sensor, or a control board timer fault tied to the rinse step."
symptoms:
  - "Wash and agitation happen normally, but the cycle stalls during rinse"
  - "The machine may pause indefinitely or show a fault code specific to rinse"
  - "Water may sit in the drum without refreshing for the rinse stage"
  - "The issue happens on multiple different load types and detergent amounts"
causes:
  - title: "Excess suds confusing the water-level sensor"
    description: "Using too much detergent, or a non-HE detergent in a high-efficiency machine, produces excess foam that can fool the pressure sensor into misreading the water level, pausing the cycle as a safety measure."
  - title: "Weak water inlet flow during rinse refill"
    description: "If your water pressure is marginal, the machine may be able to complete the initial fill but time out waiting for the rinse refill, especially if a valve or inlet screen is partially restricted."
  - title: "Control board timer or sensor fault"
    description: "Some models have a specific sequence fault tied to the rinse-stage sensor readings, unrelated to the wash or spin electronics."
  - title: "Partially clogged drain from the wash stage"
    description: "If drainage before rinse is a little slow (not enough to fully block, but enough to run long), the cycle can appear to stall as it waits longer than expected before refilling for rinse."
safeChecks:
  - "Check how much detergent you're using against the manufacturer's recommended dose, especially if using a non-HE detergent in an HE machine."
  - "Check your inlet hose screens (where the hoses connect to the machine) for debris restricting flow."
  - "Note whether the stall happens on every load or only larger/heavier ones, which can point toward drainage timing rather than a sensor fault."
  - "Check for visible suds still present in the drum when the stall occurs."
steps:
  - title: "Reduce detergent and retest"
    description: "Run a load with the manufacturer-recommended detergent amount (often less than people assume) and watch whether the rinse stage completes normally."
  - title: "Check inlet hose screens"
    description: "With the water supply off, disconnect the inlet hoses at the machine and check the small mesh screens for debris, cleaning them with a soft brush."
  - title: "Run an empty cycle to test"
    description: "An empty hot cycle with no detergent isolates whether the issue is load/suds related or a genuine mechanical/electronic fault."
  - title: "Power-cycle the machine"
    description: "Unplug for a minute and restart if you suspect a one-off control board glitch, then monitor the next full cycle closely."
dontDo:
  - "Don't add extra detergent thinking it will help clean better — it's a leading cause of exactly this stalling pattern."
  - "Don't ignore a repeated stall at the same stage — it's a useful diagnostic clue, not a random glitch, if it happens consistently."
  - "Don't keep restarting the same stalled cycle repeatedly without changing detergent amount or checking hoses first."
whenToCallPro:
  - "The stall continues even with reduced detergent, clean inlet screens, and a power-cycle reset."
  - "You suspect a control board or sensor fault specific to the rinse stage."
  - "The machine shows a specific fault code — check your manual for what it indicates before calling for service."
faqs:
  - q: "Why would suds specifically cause a problem at rinse but not wash?"
    a: "Suds tend to build up cumulatively through the wash stage and can peak right as the machine tries to sense water level for the rinse refill, which is often when the sensor first gets confused by foam rather than water."
  - q: "Does this mean my water-level sensor is broken?"
    a: "Not necessarily — try the detergent-amount and inlet-screen checks first, since those cause the same symptom without any faulty part. A genuine sensor fault is only likely if the stall continues after ruling those out."
  - q: "Is a partial drain clog from the wash stage really enough to cause this?"
    a: "Yes, in some cases — if drainage before rinse takes noticeably longer than normal without fully failing, some machines can interpret that delay as a fault and pause rather than proceeding."
