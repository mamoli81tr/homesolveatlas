quickAnswer: "If the dishwasher fills to the normal water level but you never hear or see any wash action begin, the wash pump or motor isn't engaging — this is a different, generally more serious fault than a spray arm that's simply blocked, since the whole wash system isn't starting at all rather than one arm underperforming."
symptoms:
  - "Water enters and reaches a normal level, confirmed by checking through the door or a clear panel if present"
  - "No humming, spraying, or wash-cycle sound follows the fill"
  - "The cycle may eventually proceed to drain without ever having washed"
  - "Dishes come out exactly as loaded, untouched by any spray"
causes:
  - title: "Wash motor not engaging"
    description: "The motor that drives the wash pump can fail to start due to a worn motor, a failed start capacitor/relay, or a control board not sending the signal — all of which stop wash action before it begins."
  - title: "Wash pump impeller jammed or disconnected"
    description: "Similar to a washing machine's drain pump, the wash pump's impeller can be physically jammed by debris or come loose from its shaft, leaving the motor unable to move water even if it tries to run."
  - title: "Control board fault"
    description: "The control board coordinates fill, wash, and drain — if it fails to send the wash-start signal specifically, filling can proceed normally while wash never begins."
  - title: "Door switch not confirming closed"
    description: "Some machines will fill but hold off on active washing if they don't fully register the door as securely closed, similar to how a washing machine won't spin without door confirmation."
safeChecks:
  - "Confirm the door is closing and latching fully — open and firmly reclose it, then restart the cycle."
  - "Listen closely right after fill completes for any motor sound at all, even a faint hum, which would suggest the motor is trying but not succeeding."
  - "Check your control panel for any error code or flashing indicator that might point to a specific fault."
  - "Unplug the dishwasher (or switch off at the breaker) before any closer inspection."
steps:
  - title: "Reclose the door firmly and restart"
    description: "Rule out a door-sensing issue first, since it's the simplest possible cause and needs no disassembly to check."
  - title: "Listen for motor engagement"
    description: "Run a cycle and listen carefully right as wash should begin — a faint hum with no spraying suggests the motor is trying but mechanically blocked; total silence suggests the motor or control signal isn't engaging at all."
  - title: "Check for a fault code"
    description: "Check your manual for what any displayed code means before assuming the cause — many manufacturers use specific codes for pump, motor, or door faults."
  - title: "Note the pattern for a technician"
    description: "Whether you heard a hum or total silence is valuable diagnostic information — pass this along if you need to call for service, since it substantially narrows down the likely component."
dontDo:

  - "Don't run repeated cycles hoping the wash stage will eventually start, especially if you hear the motor straining."
  - "Don't assume this is the same as a blocked spray arm — a blocked arm still means the pump and motor are working, which is a much simpler fix."

faqs:
  - q: "Is this the same problem as a spray arm not spinning?"
    a: "No — a blocked spray arm means the pump is working but water isn't reaching one area. This is about the entire wash system (pump and motor) not engaging at all, which is a more central fault."
  - q: "Why would the door affect whether washing starts?"
    a: "Many dishwashers, like washing machines, won't run active wash cycles unless they can confirm the door is securely latched, as a basic safety measure against water spraying out."
  - q: "Can I test the wash motor myself?"
    a: "This isn't recommended without experience — testing a wash motor safely involves working with electrical components in a way that goes beyond routine homeowner troubleshooting."

Because water fills normally, the inlet valve and float switch are working — this points the problem squarely at the wash system itself rather than anything upstream of it.

| What you notice | Most likely cause |
|---|---|
| Total silence after fill, no motor sound | Motor or control board not engaging |
| A faint hum but no spraying | Wash pump impeller jammed |
| Door doesn't latch fully | Door switch not confirming closed |
| An error code appears on the display | Check your manual — often points directly to the faulty component |
