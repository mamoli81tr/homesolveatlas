quickAnswer: "An oven that runs hot or cold compared to its dial setting usually needs a thermostat recalibration, which most ovens support through a hidden menu — no parts needed. A failing heating element is the next most common cause."
symptoms:
  - "Food consistently undercooked or overcooked at standard recipe times"
  - "An oven thermometer reads noticeably different from the set temperature"
  - "Uneven baking, with one side browning faster than the other"
  - "Preheating takes much longer than the manual states"
causes:
  - title: "Thermostat drift"
    description: "Oven thermostats can drift a few degrees off over years of use, which is a normal, fixable calibration issue rather than a fault."
  - title: "Failing bake or broil element"
    description: "A visibly warped, blistered, or arcing heating element isn't producing consistent heat and needs replacement."
  - title: "Faulty temperature sensor (oven probe)"
    description: "The small sensor rod inside the oven cavity reports temperature to the control board. If it's touching the oven wall or has failed, readings become inaccurate."
  - title: "Door seal not sealing properly"
    description: "A worn or torn door gasket lets heat escape, so the oven has to work harder and can undershoot the set temperature."
safeChecks:
  - "Place an independent oven thermometer on the center rack and compare its reading to the set temperature after preheating fully."
  - "Visually inspect the bake element (bottom) and broil element (top) for warping, blistering, or bright orange arcing spots."
  - "Check that the temperature sensor rod isn't touching the oven's interior wall."
  - "Run your hand near the closed oven door edges while it's on to feel for heat escaping through the seal."
steps:
  - title: "Verify the discrepancy with an oven thermometer"
    description: "Set the oven to 350°F (175°C), let it fully preheat, and compare the thermometer reading after 15 minutes."
  - title: "Recalibrate the thermostat"
    description: "Most ovens have a hidden calibration setting accessible by holding specific buttons (check your manual) that lets you adjust the displayed temperature up or down by a set number of degrees."
  - title: "Re-test after calibrating"
    description: "Repeat the thermometer test to confirm the adjustment brought the actual temperature in line with the setting."
  - title: "Inspect elements and sensor if calibration doesn't help"
    description: "If the gap remains large even after calibration, visually check the heating elements and sensor position as described in the safe checks above."
dontDo:
  - "Don't touch a heating element while the oven is on or still hot — allow it to cool fully first."
  - "Don't attempt to bend the temperature sensor back into position with the power on."
  - "Don't repeatedly recalibrate by large amounts hoping to mask a failing element — this treats the symptom, not the cause."
whenToCallPro:
  - "The heating element shows visible damage, arcing, or has stopped glowing during preheat."
  - "Calibration doesn't close the gap between set and actual temperature."
  - "You smell gas near a gas oven at any point — leave the area and contact your gas utility immediately."
  - "The door seal is torn and heat loss is significant."
faqs:
  - q: "How much can I adjust an oven's calibration?"
    a: "Most manufacturers allow adjustments of up to 35°F (about 20°C) in either direction. If your oven is off by more than that, a component issue is more likely than simple drift."
  - q: "Can I replace a bake element myself?"
    a: "On many electric ovens, the bake element is a simple screw-in, unplug part designed for DIY replacement — always unplug or de-energize the oven first and consult your model's manual."
  - q: "Why does my oven bake unevenly even at the right temperature?"
    a: "Uneven baking is often about airflow and rack position rather than temperature accuracy — try the center rack and avoid overcrowding pans, which blocks convection."
