quickAnswer: "A leak that shows up specifically during spin, and not during fill or wash, points to something that only becomes a problem under the pressure and vibration of high-speed spinning: a worn door seal (front-loaders), excess suds being forced out, or a hose connection that's gradually loosened from vibration."
symptoms:
  - "No visible leak during fill, wash, or rinse — water only appears during or right after spin"
  - "Water pooling near the front of the machine (front-loaders) or underneath"
  - "The leak may be worse with larger loads or higher spin speeds"
  - "Suds may be visible in the leaked water, suggesting an oversudsing component"
causes:
  - title: "Worn or torn door seal (front-loaders)"
    description: "The rubber door gasket can develop small tears or lose its shape over time. Under normal water levels it may seal fine, but the pressure and movement of high-speed spin can force water through a weak spot."
  - title: "Excess suds forced out under pressure"
    description: "Too much detergent, or the wrong type for your machine, can create suds that get forced past seals during the high-agitation spin stage even if the water level itself is normal."
  - title: "Hose connection loosened by vibration"
    description: "Repeated vibration from spin cycles can gradually loosen a hose clamp or fitting that was otherwise secure, causing a leak that only appears under that specific vibration."
  - title: "Overfilled or unbalanced load pressing against the door seal"
    description: "A very full load can press unevenly against the door seal during the tumbling of spin, creating gaps that let water escape that wouldn't otherwise leak."
steps:
  - title: "Reduce detergent and retest"
    description: "Run a load with less detergent (or switch to an HE formula if you aren't using one) to rule out oversudsing as the cause."
  - title: "Inspect the door seal closely"
    description: "On a front-loader, run your hand around the full fold of the door gasket, feeling for tears, stiffness, or debris preventing a full seal, especially in the lower fold where water and debris collect."
  - title: "Check hose connections after unplugging"
    description: "With the machine unplugged and water supply off, check that hose clamps and fittings at both the machine and the wall are still snug — vibration can loosen them gradually over months."
  - title: "Reduce load size and retest"
    description: "Try a smaller, better-balanced load through a full cycle to see if the leak still appears, which helps isolate an overloading contribution."
safeChecks:
  - "Check detergent dose against the manufacturer's recommendation before assuming a mechanical fault."
  - "Look for suds specifically in the leaked water, which points toward oversudsing rather than a seal or hose issue."
  - "Note exactly when in the cycle the leak appears — during spin specifically, versus throughout, changes the likely cause significantly."
dontDo:
  - "Don't ignore a door-seal leak thinking it's minor — repeated water exposure to the same spot on the floor or cabinet can cause water damage over time."
  - "Don't overtighten hose clamps trying to over-correct — this can crack fittings, especially plastic ones."
  - "Don't keep running large loads if you suspect the load size itself is contributing to the leak."
whenToCallPro:
  - "The door seal shows a visible tear or damage and needs replacing."
  - "Hose connections are confirmed tight but the leak continues."
  - "You can't identify where exactly the water is coming from despite checking the seal, hoses, and detergent amount."
faqs:
  - q: "Why would a leak only happen during spin and not the rest of the cycle?"
    a: "Spin is the highest-pressure, highest-vibration stage of a wash cycle. A weak spot in a seal, a slightly loose hose connection, or excess suds can all stay contained under normal conditions but get pushed past their limit specifically under that stress."
  - q: "Can too much detergent really cause a leak, not just extra suds?"
    a: "Yes — suds forced out past a door seal or vent during high-speed spin carry water with them, so what starts as 'just foam' can turn into a real puddle on the floor."
  - q: "Is a torn door seal an expensive repair?"
    a: "It's a defined, replaceable part, though cost and difficulty vary by model — get a quote before deciding between repair and considering the machine's overall age and condition."
