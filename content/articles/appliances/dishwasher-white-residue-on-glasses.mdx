quickAnswer: "White residue that wipes or rinses off with a little vinegar is hard-water mineral film — fixable with rinse aid and, if needed, a dishwasher cleaner. A cloudy haze that won't wipe off and feels rough is permanent glass etching from too much detergent or heat over time, which can't be reversed."
symptoms:
  - "A chalky white film coating glasses and dishes after washing"
  - "Spots or streaks that are worse on glassware than on plates"
  - "Residue that feels gritty or leaves white marks when you run a finger over it"
  - "Problem is worse in areas with known hard water"
causes:
  - title: "Hard-water mineral deposits"
    description: "Calcium and magnesium in hard water leave a white film on dishes as the water evaporates, especially without a rinse aid to help water sheet off cleanly."
  - title: "Insufficient rinse aid"
    description: "Rinse aid reduces water's surface tension so it sheets off dishes instead of forming droplets that dry into spots — running low or empty is a common cause."
  - title: "Water too cool during the wash cycle"
    description: "Detergent and rinse aid both work best at higher temperatures; if your home's hot water supply runs cooler than ideal, minerals and detergent residue are more likely to remain on dishes."
  - title: "Permanent glass etching (not residue)"
    description: "Repeated exposure to too much detergent, water that's too soft (over-softened), or high heat gradually erodes the glass surface itself — this looks similar to mineral film but doesn't wipe off, because it's physical damage, not a coating."
safeChecks:
  - "Try wiping a hazy glass with a bit of white vinegar on a cloth — if the cloudiness comes off, it's mineral residue; if it doesn't, it's likely etching."
  - "Check your rinse aid dispenser and refill it if low or empty."
  - "Check your dishwasher detergent dose against the package instructions — more isn't better and can contribute to both residue and etching over time."
steps:
  - title: "Test with vinegar"
    description: "Wipe a cloudy glass with white vinegar. If the haze disappears, proceed with the steps below for mineral residue."
  - title: "Refill rinse aid and re-run"
    description: "Fill the rinse aid dispenser fully and run a normal cycle — this is the single most effective fix for ongoing mineral spotting."
  - title: "Run a dishwasher cleaner cycle"
    description: "Run an empty hot cycle with a dishwasher-safe cleaner (or a cup of white vinegar in a bowl on the top rack) to clear built-up mineral scale from the machine's interior and spray arms."
  - title: "Adjust detergent dose"
    description: "If you're using more detergent than the package recommends 'to be safe,' reduce it to the recommended amount — excess detergent is a common contributor to both film and etching."
dontDo:
  - "Don't add extra detergent thinking it will clean better — it's one of the leading causes of both mineral film and permanent etching."
  - "Don't confuse etching with residue and keep trying rinse aid or vinegar fixes that won't work on physical glass damage."
  - "Don't run the dishwasher with the rinse aid dispenser empty for extended periods in a hard-water area."
whenToCallPro:
  - "You've confirmed it's mineral residue but rinse aid and a cleaning cycle don't improve it — a plumber or appliance technician can check your home's water hardness and hot water temperature."
  - "You want a water softener installed to address hard water at the source rather than symptom by symptom."
faqs:
  - q: "Can etched glasses be fixed?"
    a: "No — etching is permanent physical erosion of the glass surface, not a coating. Reducing detergent amount and water hardness going forward can prevent it from getting worse, but existing etching won't reverse."
  - q: "Does rinse aid really make a big difference?"
    a: "Yes — it's specifically designed to prevent water spotting by helping water sheet off dishes rather than forming droplets that dry into mineral spots, and is one of the most effective single fixes for residue."
  - q: "Is hard water bad for my dishwasher itself, not just my glasses?"
    a: "Yes — mineral scale from hard water can build up inside the machine's spray arms and heating element over time, which is why a periodic dishwasher-cleaner cycle helps beyond just glassware."
