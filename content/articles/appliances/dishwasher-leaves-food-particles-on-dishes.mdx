quickAnswer: "Visible chunks or bits of food left on dishes after a full cycle almost always come down to one of three things, roughly in order of likelihood: a dirty filter no longer catching and breaking down debris properly, dishes loaded too closely together blocking spray coverage, or a partially blocked spray arm. Checking all three in one sitting resolves the large majority of cases."
symptoms:
  - "Visible pieces of food stuck to plates, bowls, or utensils after a normal cycle"
  - "The problem is worse with certain foods (rice, egg, oatmeal) that dry hard"
  - "Redeposited debris appears on items that were otherwise clean going in"
  - "The issue has gradually worsened over recent weeks or months"
causes:
  - title: "Dirty or clogged filter"
    description: "A filter overdue for cleaning is the single most common cause — it stops effectively catching and breaking down food debris, which then recirculates and redeposits onto dishes instead of washing away."
  - title: "Overloading or improper loading"
    description: "Dishes loaded too close together block water and spray coverage from reaching all surfaces, leaving debris in the gaps and shadowed areas."
  - title: "Blocked or clogged spray arm"
    description: "The same nozzle-clog and obstruction issues that cause zone-specific cleaning problems can also cause a general, milder version of this symptom across the whole load."
  - title: "Water temperature too low"
    description: "Dried-on or greasy food needs adequate heat to break down and rinse away — consistently cool water can leave more residue than usual even with everything else working."
safeChecks:
  - "Remove and inspect the filter — if you can't remember the last time you cleaned it, this is very likely a contributing factor."
  - "Check how dishes are loaded, looking for items nested closely together or blocking each other."
  - "Check both spray arms for visible clogs or obstruction."
  - "Consider whether you've recently changed detergent, load habits, or noticed a change in water temperature."
steps:
  - title: "Clean the filter thoroughly"
    description: "Remove the filter assembly and rinse thoroughly under hot water, using an old toothbrush on any trapped debris — this alone resolves many cases."
  - title: "Reload with better spacing"
    description: "Space items so water can reach all surfaces, avoiding nesting bowls or plates directly against each other."
  - title: "Check both spray arms"
    description: "Follow our spray arm guide to remove, inspect, and clear both the upper and lower arms."
  - title: "Scrape (don't fully pre-rinse) before loading"
    description: "Scraping off large food chunks helps, but full pre-rinsing can actually reduce cleaning performance on machines with soil sensors, which rely on some soil to calibrate the wash properly."
dontDo:
  - "Don't let the filter go uncleaned for months — it's designed to need regular attention, not a one-time setup step."
  - "Don't overcrowd racks trying to fit in a full day's dishes at once, which blocks spray coverage broadly."
  - "Don't assume you need a stronger detergent before checking the filter and loading pattern first."
whenToCallPro:
  - "The filter is clean, loading is reasonable, and both spray arms are clear, but food particles are still a consistent problem."
  - "You suspect reduced water temperature or pressure affecting overall wash performance."
  - "The problem has developed alongside other symptoms like unusual noise or a spray arm not spinning."
faqs:
  - q: "How often should I clean the filter?"
    a: "Once a month for average use is a reasonable baseline, more often if you rarely scrape plates or wash a lot of starchy or fibrous foods that shed debris."
  - q: "Should I rinse dishes completely before loading?"
    a: "A light scrape to remove large chunks is enough for most modern dishwashers — full pre-rinsing can actually reduce cleaning performance on machines that use a soil sensor to calibrate the wash."
  - q: "Is this the same as a gritty or sandy feeling on dishes?"
    a: "No — visible food particles are chunks or bits of food, while a gritty or sandy feel is a different, finer texture usually tied to filter damage or hard water. See our dedicated guide on that specific symptom."

Because this symptom usually spreads across the whole load rather than favoring one rack or zone, the filter — which affects the entire wash system — is the most likely single cause and the best place to start.

| What you notice | Most likely cause |
|---|---|
| Debris across the whole load, not one zone | Dirty filter |
| Debris mainly in gaps between crowded dishes | Overloading blocking spray coverage |
| Debris concentrated on one rack | See our rack-specific guides instead |
| Worse with starchy or greasy foods specifically | Water temperature too low |
