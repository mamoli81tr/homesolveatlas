quickAnswer: "If your washer fills and agitates but the spin cycle never kicks in, the most common cause is an unbalanced load tripping a safety sensor, followed by a failed lid or door switch, and — on older top-loaders — a worn drive belt or motor coupling."
symptoms:
  - "Wash and rinse work normally but the final spin never starts"
  - "Drum stays full of water at the end of the cycle"
  - "A faint humming sound during what should be the spin stage"
  - "The cycle pauses or restarts repeatedly near the end"
causes:
  - title: "Unbalanced load"
    description: "A load bunched to one side (common with a single heavy item like a comforter) trips a safety sensor that blocks spinning to prevent the machine from shaking itself apart."
  - title: "Failed lid or door switch"
    description: "The machine won't spin — as a safety measure — if it can't confirm the lid or door is securely closed and latched, even if it looks closed to you."
  - title: "Worn drive belt (top-loaders)"
    description: "Older belt-driven washers can agitate fine on a worn belt but not have enough grip left to reach spin speed."
  - title: "Worn motor coupling (some top-loaders)"
    description: "A small plastic coupling between the motor and transmission is designed to fail before more expensive parts do, disconnecting spin function while agitation still works."
safeChecks:
  - "Redistribute the load evenly around the drum and restart the spin cycle."
  - "Open and firmly reclose the lid or door, listening for a clear latch click."
  - "Reduce the load size and try a spin-only cycle to see if a smaller load spins normally."
  - "Unplug the machine before inspecting any belt or coupling underneath or behind the cabinet."
steps:
  - title: "Rebalance and retry"
    description: "Redistribute a lopsided load and run the spin cycle again — this alone resolves the majority of no-spin complaints."
  - title: "Test the lid/door switch"
    description: "Close the lid or door firmly and confirm it latches. On front-loaders, check the door seal isn't trapping fabric and preventing a full seal."
  - title: "Run a smaller test load"
    description: "A small, evenly distributed load isolates whether the issue is balance-related or mechanical."
  - title: "Inspect the belt or coupling"
    description: "On a top-loader, unplug the machine and check (or have a technician check) the drive belt for looseness or cracking, and the motor coupling for stripped plastic teeth."
dontDo:
  - "Don't run repeated full-load spin cycles hoping an unbalanced load will 'work itself out' — this strains the motor and suspension."
  - "Don't bypass or tape down a lid/door switch to force a spin cycle — it's a safety interlock."
  - "Don't ignore a burning-rubber smell, which points to a slipping belt under load."
whenToCallPro:
  - "The load is balanced and the lid/door switch checks out, but spin still won't start."
  - "You can see a visibly loose, cracked, or broken drive belt."
  - "The machine makes a grinding or clunking sound specifically during the spin attempt."
  - "This is a direct-drive washer (no belt) that agitates but won't spin — this usually points to the motor control board."
faqs:
  - q: "Why does my washer agitate fine but not spin?"
    a: "Agitation and spin are separate functions with separate failure points. A worn belt, failed coupling, or a safety switch can each block spin specifically while leaving agitation unaffected."
  - q: "Can one heavy item really stop the whole spin cycle?"
    a: "Yes — a single heavy, bunched item like a comforter or rug is one of the most common causes of an unbalanced-load spin lockout, especially in high-efficiency machines with sensitive balance sensors."
  - q: "Is a motor coupling expensive to replace?"
    a: "It's usually one of the cheaper washer parts, and is specifically designed as a 'sacrificial' component that fails before the motor or transmission does — but installation still typically requires removing the cabinet."
sources:
  - "Error code meanings and part locations vary by model. Check your washer's manual before opening any panel."
safetyWarning: "Always unplug the washer before removing any access panel or inspecting a belt or coupling. If you smell burning rubber or see smoke, stop immediately and disconnect power at the wall."
