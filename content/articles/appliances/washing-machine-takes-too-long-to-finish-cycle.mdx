quickAnswer: "Some added length is normal — many modern washers use sensors to adjust cycle time based on load size and soil level, and a full cold-water load can genuinely take longer than older fixed-time machines. A cycle that keeps extending well beyond its original estimate usually means low water pressure, excess suds, or the machine repeatedly re-attempting to correct an unbalanced load."
symptoms:
  - "The estimated time on the display keeps increasing rather than counting down steadily"
  - "The cycle takes noticeably longer than it used to on the same settings"
  - "Extra rinse stages seem to be added automatically"
  - "The machine pauses periodically, especially before or during spin"
causes:
  - title: "Sensor-based cycle adjustment (often normal)"
    description: "Many modern washers monitor soil level, load size, and water clarity, and will extend wash or add rinses automatically — this is by design, not a fault, though it can surprise users switching from an older machine."
  - title: "Low water pressure or a partially restricted inlet"
    description: "Slow filling adds time at every fill stage of the cycle (wash, each rinse), and a partially clogged inlet screen or genuinely low household pressure can add up to a much longer total cycle."
  - title: "Repeated imbalance correction attempts"
    description: "If a load is unbalanced, many machines will add water and slowly redistribute it, retrying several times before proceeding to spin — each attempt adds real time to the cycle."
  - title: "Excess suds triggering extra rinses"
    description: "Too much detergent can trigger a machine's sensors to add extra rinse cycles automatically to fully clear the suds, extending total time meaningfully."
safeChecks:
  - "Check your detergent dose against the manufacturer's recommendation, especially if you've recently switched detergent brands or types."
  - "Check inlet hose screens for restriction if fill stages specifically seem slow."
  - "Note whether the extension happens with every load or specifically with large or unevenly distributed ones, which points toward imbalance correction."
  - "Compare against your model's normal cycle time range in the manual — some 'long' cycles are actually within normal spec for sensor-based models."
steps:
  - title: "Check detergent amount"
    description: "Reduce to the manufacturer-recommended dose and see if cycle time normalizes on the next load."
  - title: "Load more evenly"
    description: "Distribute laundry evenly around the drum rather than piling it to one side, reducing the chance of repeated imbalance-correction delays."
  - title: "Check inlet screens and water pressure"
    description: "If fill stages specifically seem slow, check the inlet hose screens for debris and confirm normal water pressure at another tap in the house."
  - title: "Compare against your model's specified range"
    description: "Check your manual for the expected cycle time range for your most-used settings — sensor-based machines can have a wide legitimate range depending on load."
dontDo:
  - "Don't assume a longer cycle than you remember automatically means something is broken — many modern machines are simply designed this way."
  - "Don't keep adding detergent 'to be safe' if cycles seem to be adding extra rinses — this usually makes it worse, not better."
  - "Don't ignore genuinely low household water pressure, since it affects every water-using appliance, not just the washer."
whenToCallPro:
  - "Cycle times have been getting progressively longer over weeks or months with no change in detergent or load habits."
  - "The machine repeatedly pauses and seems stuck rather than just running a longer sensor-adjusted cycle."
  - "You've ruled out detergent, load balance, and water pressure, and cycles are still unusually long."
faqs:
  - q: "Is it normal for cycles to just take longer than they used to?"
    a: "It can be — many newer washers use sensors to tailor cycle length to the load, and cold-water or eco cycles in particular are often genuinely longer than older fixed-time machines. Check your model's expected range before assuming a fault."
  - q: "Does using less detergent really shorten the cycle?"
    a: "It can, if excess suds have been triggering automatic extra rinses — using the manufacturer-recommended amount often resolves both the suds and the added time."
  - q: "Can low water pressure really add that much time?"
    a: "Yes, especially on machines that fill multiple times per cycle (initial wash plus one or more rinses) — each fill stage takes longer, and that adds up across a full cycle."
