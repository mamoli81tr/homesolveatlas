quickAnswer: "Water on the floor in front of or under a refrigerator is almost always a clogged interior defrost drain (water backs up and overflows inside, then leaks out the front) or a loose ice-maker water line connection at the back — not a refrigerant leak, which wouldn't produce liquid water at all."
symptoms:
  - "A puddle forms under the front of the fridge, often near the crisper drawers"
  - "Water appears gradually over hours rather than all at once"
  - "Ice buildup visible at the back wall of the freezer or fridge compartment"
  - "Puddle reappears within a day or two of being wiped up"
causes:
  - title: "Clogged defrost drain"
    description: "A small drain at the back of the fridge compartment carries water from the automatic defrost cycle to a pan underneath. Food debris or ice can block it, causing water to back up and spill out the front instead."
  - title: "Loose or damaged water line connection"
    description: "On models with an ice maker or water dispenser, the plastic water line connection at the back can loosen, kink, or crack over time, leaking at the connection point."
  - title: "Clogged or misaligned drain pan"
    description: "The pan underneath the fridge that collects normal defrost water can crack, sit unlevel, or overflow if the drain above it is clogged."
  - title: "Door seal not sealing fully"
    description: "A worn or dirty door gasket lets warm, humid air in, which can accelerate frost buildup and indirectly overwhelm the defrost drain."
safeChecks:
  - "Check the back wall inside the fridge and freezer compartments for visible ice buildup that could indicate a drain blockage."
  - "Look at the water line connection behind the fridge (if it has an ice maker) for visible dripping or a loose fitting."
  - "Check the drain pan underneath the fridge (usually accessible by removing a lower front grille) for cracks or overflow."
  - "Run a finger along the door gasket to check it's making full contact and isn't torn."
steps:
  - title: "Clear the defrost drain"
    description: "Locate the small drain hole at the back of the fridge or freezer compartment (check your manual for the exact spot) and flush it with warm water using a turkey baster or funnel to clear debris."
  - title: "Check the water line connection"
    description: "With the fridge unplugged and water supply off, inspect the line where it connects at the back of the unit and at the wall shutoff valve, tightening or replacing the fitting if it's loose or cracked."
  - title: "Inspect and clean the drain pan"
    description: "Remove the lower front grille (usually snaps or unscrews off) to access the drain pan, and clean out any standing water or debris."
  - title: "Check the door seal"
    description: "Clean the gasket with warm soapy water and check it seals fully around the whole door — a poor seal contributes to the ice buildup that clogs the drain in the first place."
dontDo:
  - "Don't ignore a recurring puddle thinking it will resolve on its own — standing water can damage flooring and promote mold underneath the fridge."
  - "Don't use a sharp tool to clear the defrost drain — a turkey baster, pipe cleaner, or warm water flush is safer and won't puncture anything."
  - "Don't assume a leak means a refrigerant problem — refrigerant leaks don't produce liquid water and have a completely different set of symptoms (loss of cooling, no water)."
whenToCallPro:
  - "The leak continues after clearing the drain, checking the water line, and inspecting the pan."
  - "You find a cracked drain pan or water line that needs a replacement part."
  - "Water appears to be coming from inside the refrigerator's insulation or wall rather than the drain system."
  - "The fridge is also failing to cool properly in addition to leaking."
faqs:
  - q: "Is a leaking refrigerator dangerous?"
    a: "Not electrically dangerous in most cases since the water source is usually the defrost or water-line system rather than exposed wiring, but standing water is a slip hazard and can damage flooring if left unaddressed."
  - q: "How often should I check the defrost drain?"
    a: "It's not something that needs regular scheduled maintenance, but if you notice recurring water or ice buildup at the back wall, that's the signal to check and clear it."
  - q: "Can hard water cause the ice maker line to clog?"
    a: "Yes — mineral buildup from hard water can narrow or clog the water line over time, which can contribute to both leaking at connections and reduced ice production."
sources:
  - "Check your refrigerator's manual for the exact defrost drain and water line locations, since they vary between French-door, side-by-side, and top-freezer models."
safetyWarning: "Always unplug the refrigerator and shut off its water supply line before working on the water line connection. Never use sharp tools near the drain or water lines."

Where the water shows up first is the fastest way to narrow this down.

| What you notice | Likely direction | Safe next check |
|---|---|---|
| Puddle appears gradually under the front, ice visible at the back wall | Clogged defrost drain | Flush the drain hole with warm water using a turkey baster |
| Dripping specifically at the back of the unit, near the water line | Loose or damaged ice-maker water line connection | Check the fitting at the wall shutoff and at the back of the fridge |
| Water pools under the unit itself, not the front | Cracked or misaligned drain pan | Remove the lower front grille and inspect the pan |
| Leaking plus the fridge also isn't cooling properly | Not a simple drain or line issue | This combination is worth a technician visit rather than more DIY troubleshooting |
