quickAnswer: "Fabric softener (also called fabric conditioner) that stays sitting in the dispenser tray after a cycle almost always means the small siphon channel is clogged with dried residue, or the softener itself is too thick/undiluted to trigger the automatic release — both are cleared with a thorough dispenser cleaning."
symptoms:
  - "Softener remains visibly sitting in the dispenser compartment after the cycle finishes"
  - "Clothes don't smell or feel like softener was used, even though it was added"
  - "A buildup of dried, gel-like residue is visible in the dispenser drawer"
  - "The problem is more noticeable with concentrated or thicker softener brands"
causes:
  - title: "Clogged siphon channel"
    description: "The softener compartment releases its contents via a small siphon mechanism that triggers once water reaches a certain level — dried softener residue building up over time can block this channel."
  - title: "Softener too thick or added undiluted"
    description: "Some softeners are concentrated enough that, especially in a compartment with reduced water flow, they don't dilute and release properly through the siphon."
  - title: "Using the wrong compartment"
    description: "It's an easy mix-up on some drawer layouts — check that softener is going into the compartment specifically marked for it, not the main or pre-wash detergent section."
  - title: "Low water pressure or a short wash cycle"
    description: "Some quick or eco cycles use less water in the dispenser flush, which combined with a partially clogged siphon can be enough to prevent full release."
steps:
  - title: "Remove and thoroughly clean the dispenser drawer"
    description: "Most drawers pull out fully or partially — soak it in warm water and use an old toothbrush to clear dried residue from all channels and compartments."
  - title: "Clean the siphon area inside the machine"
    description: "With the drawer removed, use a small brush or damp cloth to clean the cavity and channel the drawer sits in, since residue often builds up there too, not just on the drawer itself."
  - title: "Dilute thick softener if needed"
    description: "If you use a concentrated softener, try diluting it with a small amount of water in the compartment to help it trigger the siphon more reliably."
  - title: "Confirm the correct compartment"
    description: "Check your machine's drawer labeling (often a flower or droplet icon for softener) to confirm you're using the intended compartment."
safeChecks:
  - "Check the compartment icons/labels to confirm softener is going in the correct slot."
  - "Run a rinse-only cycle with just water in the softener compartment to see if it's your specific softener brand triggering the issue."
  - "Look for visible dried residue buildup, which is the most common and easiest-to-fix cause."
dontDo:
  - "Don't add softener directly to the drum instead of the dispenser as a workaround — undiluted softener can stain clothes if it contacts fabric directly."
  - "Don't use excessive softener trying to compensate for a partial release — this often makes buildup and clogging worse over time."
  - "Don't ignore a consistently clogged dispenser — dried residue buildup tends to worsen, not improve, without a proper cleaning."
whenToCallPro:
  - "The dispenser and siphon area are both thoroughly cleaned but softener still doesn't release."
  - "You suspect a mechanical fault with the water flush valve that fills the dispenser drawer, rather than a clog."
faqs:
  - q: "Is fabric conditioner the same as fabric softener?"
    a: "Yes — fabric conditioner and fabric softener are two names for the same laundry product; conditioner is the more common term in the UK and several other regions. Everything in this guide applies the same way regardless of which term is on your bottle."
  - q: "How often should I clean the dispenser drawer?"
    a: "Every 1–2 months is a reasonable baseline for most households, more often if you regularly use thicker or concentrated softeners that leave more residue."
  - q: "Can I just pour softener straight into the drum?"
    a: "It's best avoided — the dispenser exists to dilute and release softener at the right point in the cycle. Undiluted softener applied directly to fabric can leave greasy stains."
  - q: "Does the type of softener matter?"
    a: "Yes — thicker, more concentrated formulas are more prone to clogging a siphon-based dispenser than standard-dilution softeners, especially if the dispenser isn't cleaned regularly."
