quickAnswer: "If the fan runs and you feel airflow but it's not cold, the top causes are a dirty air filter restricting airflow, a frozen or dirty evaporator coil, or a unit that's undersized for the room and running constantly without ever catching up."
symptoms:
  - "Fan runs and air blows out, but it's room-temperature or only slightly cool"
  - "The unit runs continuously without ever seeming to reach the set temperature"
  - "Airflow feels weaker than when the unit was new"
  - "Ice visible on the front coil when you remove the filter/cover"
causes:
  - title: "Dirty air filter"
    description: "A clogged filter restricts airflow across the cold coil, reducing how much cool air actually reaches the room — this is the most common and easiest cause to fix."
  - title: "Frozen or dirty evaporator coil"
    description: "Reduced airflow (often from that same dirty filter) can cause the coil to ice over, which then blocks airflow even further in a worsening cycle."
  - title: "Undersized unit for the room"
    description: "A unit with too few BTU for the room's size will run constantly and still struggle to reach the set temperature, especially in direct sun or a room with poor insulation."
  - title: "Blocked or dirty outdoor-facing coil"
    description: "Dust, debris, or nearby plants blocking the exterior-facing coil reduce the unit's ability to reject heat outside, directly reducing cooling performance."
safeChecks:
  - "Remove and inspect the air filter — if it's visibly grey or clogged, that's very likely a major contributor."
  - "Check the front coil (behind the filter) for visible ice buildup."
  - "Confirm the unit's BTU rating against your room's approximate size — a unit sized well below the room's needs will underperform even in perfect condition."
  - "Check that the exterior-facing side isn't blocked by curtains, furniture, or outdoor debris."
steps:
  - title: "Clean or replace the filter"
    description: "Remove the filter and wash it (if reusable) or replace it (if disposable), then let it dry fully before reinstalling."
  - title: "Thaw a frozen coil if present"
    description: "Turn the unit off (fan-only mode if available, or fully off) and let any ice on the coil melt fully — this can take a few hours — before resuming normal cooling operation."
  - title: "Clear both the room-facing and outdoor-facing airflow paths"
    description: "Make sure nothing blocks air intake or exhaust on either side of the unit, indoors or outdoors."
  - title: "Reassess sizing if problems persist"
    description: "If the unit runs constantly without reaching temperature even with a clean filter and clear coil, use a BTU calculator to check whether it's actually sized appropriately for the room."
dontDo:
  - "Don't run the unit continuously while a coil is visibly frozen — this can eventually damage the compressor."
  - "Don't try to chip ice off the coil with a sharp tool — let it melt naturally with the unit off."
  - "Don't assume 'weak cooling' always means low refrigerant — a dirty filter or coil is a far more common and cheaper-to-fix cause."
whenToCallPro:
  - "The filter is clean, the coil isn't frozen, and cooling is still weak."
  - "You suspect a refrigerant leak (very gradual decline in cooling over a long period, with a hissing sound) — this needs EPA-certified handling."
  - "The compressor makes unusual grinding or knocking noises."
faqs:
  - q: "How often should I clean a window AC filter?"
    a: "Every 2–4 weeks during regular use is a good baseline, more often in dusty environments or homes with pets."
  - q: "Can a window unit be 'too small' even if it seems to run fine?"
    a: "Yes — an undersized unit will run constantly trying to reach the set temperature, using more energy and wearing out faster, even though it's technically 'working' the whole time."
  - q: "Is it normal for a window AC to ice up occasionally?"
    a: "Occasional light frost in very humid conditions can happen, but regular or heavy ice buildup points to restricted airflow (usually a dirty filter) that should be addressed rather than left to recur."
