The belt smell and a motor smell come from different physical mechanisms, which is why they smell — and behave — differently. A belt smell is friction heat: rubber sliding against a roller that isn't turning freely, so it tends to build up gradually the longer you keep vacuuming, getting stronger rather than appearing all at once. A motor or electrical smell tends to appear more suddenly and is often paired with the vacuum feeling noticeably hot near the motor housing itself, not just near the base where the brush roll sits. That distinction matters practically: a gradually building rubbery smell is very likely to resolve once you clear the jam and check the belt yourself. A sudden, hot, electrical smell is the one to treat as a stop-and-inspect situation rather than a routine brush-roll check.
