causes:
  - title: "Worn weatherstripping and gaps"
    description: "Weatherstripping around doors and windows compresses and wears over years, letting cold air in and warm air out even when everything looks closed."
  - title: "A heating system that hasn't been serviced"
    description: "Furnaces and boilers lose efficiency and reliability without annual maintenance, and are more likely to fail exactly when you need them most."
  - title: "Exposed pipes in unheated spaces"
    description: "Pipes running through garages, crawlspaces, or exterior walls are vulnerable to freezing and bursting during hard freezes."
  - title: "Clogged gutters"
    description: "Leaves and debris left in gutters can cause ice dams, forcing meltwater back under roof shingles and into the home."
safeChecks:
  - "Run a hand around window and door frames on a cold day to feel for draft points."
  - "Check your furnace or boiler's last service date and schedule a tune-up if it's been over a year."
  - "Locate any pipes running through unheated spaces like a garage, crawlspace, or exterior wall."
  - "Look up at your gutters (from the ground, or safely from a ladder if you're comfortable) to check for visible buildup."
steps:
  - title: "Seal drafts"
    description: "Replace worn weatherstripping around doors and windows, and use caulk on any small gaps in trim or siding where air visibly leaks."
  - title: "Schedule a heating system service"
    description: "Book a professional furnace or boiler tune-up before the cold sets in — this is the single most effective step for reliability and efficiency."
  - title: "Insulate exposed pipes"
    description: "Wrap pipes in unheated spaces with foam pipe insulation, and know where your main water shutoff valve is in case of an emergency."
  - title: "Clean gutters and downspouts"
    description: "Clear leaves and debris so meltwater drains freely away from the roof and foundation instead of pooling or freezing."
  - title: "Reverse ceiling fans"
    description: "Switch ceiling fans to run clockwise on low speed, which pushes warm air that collects near the ceiling back down into the room."
dontDo:

  - "Don't ignore small drafts thinking they're insignificant — they add up to a meaningfully higher heating bill over a full winter."
