quickAnswer: "A breaker tripping is a safety feature working correctly, not a fault in itself. The three most common causes are circuit overload, a short circuit, and a ground fault — and identifying which one is happening determines what's safe to do next."
symptoms:
  - "A specific breaker trips whenever certain appliances run together"
  - "A breaker trips immediately or within seconds of being reset"
  - "One outlet or room consistently loses power"
  - "A burning smell near the electrical panel or an outlet"
causes:
  - title: "Circuit overload"
    description: "Too many devices drawing power on the same circuit exceed the breaker's rated capacity, so it trips to prevent overheating wires. This is the most common and least serious cause."
  - title: "Short circuit"
    description: "A hot wire touching a neutral wire causes a sudden surge in current, tripping the breaker immediately, often with a noticeable snap or spark at the outlet or device."
  - title: "Ground fault"
    description: "A hot wire touching a ground wire or a grounded metal surface (common with appliances near water) causes a similar rapid trip, and is why kitchens and bathrooms use GFCI outlets."
  - title: "A failing appliance"
    description: "An aging appliance motor or heating element can draw excess current as it fails, tripping the breaker on its own circuit even without other devices running."
safeChecks:
  - "Note exactly which breaker trips and what was running at the time — this is the single most useful diagnostic clue."
  - "Unplug everything on the affected circuit before resetting the breaker, then reintroduce devices one at a time."
  - "Check for a burning smell, discoloration, or warmth at the outlet or panel — if present, don't reset the breaker."
  - "Confirm the breaker fully resets to the 'on' position rather than sitting in a middle 'tripped' position."
steps:
  - title: "Identify the affected circuit"
    description: "Check your panel's labeling (or map it if unlabeled) to know what the tripping breaker controls."
  - title: "Reduce the load"
    description: "Unplug some devices on that circuit, especially high-draw items like space heaters, hair dryers, or microwaves running simultaneously with other appliances."
  - title: "Reset the breaker"
    description: "Push the breaker fully to 'off' first, then firmly to 'on'. A single, confident click confirms a proper reset."
  - title: "Reintroduce devices gradually"
    description: "Turn devices back on one at a time, waiting a minute between each, to identify if one specific item is the trigger."
  - title: "Stop and call an electrician if it trips instantly"
    description: "If the breaker trips again immediately with nothing plugged in, this points to a wiring issue rather than an overload, and needs professional diagnosis."
dontDo:
  - "Don't repeatedly reset a breaker that trips instantly — this indicates a short or ground fault, not overload, and repeated resetting doesn't fix the underlying issue."
  - "Don't replace a breaker with a higher-amperage one to 'stop it tripping' — this defeats the safety protection and is a fire risk."
  - "Don't touch the panel with wet hands or ignore a burning smell near it."
whenToCallPro:
  - "A breaker trips instantly, with nothing plugged into that circuit."
  - "You notice scorch marks, a burning smell, or warmth at the panel or an outlet."
  - "The same breaker trips frequently even after reducing the load on that circuit."
  - "You're not fully confident identifying which circuit a breaker controls or how to safely test it."
faqs:
  - q: "Is it dangerous to reset a tripped breaker myself?"
    a: "Resetting a breaker that tripped from a simple overload is generally safe. The risk comes from repeatedly resetting one that trips instantly, which can mean an active short or fault that needs a licensed electrician."
  - q: "Why does my breaker only trip when I use the microwave and toaster together?"
    a: "This is a classic overload symptom — both appliances draw significant current, and running them on the same circuit at once exceeds what that breaker is rated to carry safely."
  - q: "What's the difference between a GFCI trip and a breaker trip?"
    a: "A GFCI outlet trips locally at the outlet (a small reset button pops out) in response to a ground fault, while a full circuit breaker trips at the panel and cuts power to everything on that circuit."
