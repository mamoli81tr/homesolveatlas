quickAnswer: "If the drum tumbles normally but there's no heat, a blown thermal fuse is the most common cause on Maytag dryers, almost always triggered by a clogged exhaust vent overheating the unit. A tripped breaker (electric) or closed gas valve (gas) are the other common causes."
symptoms:
  - "The drum tumbles and the cycle runs its full time, but clothes come out cold"
  - "No warm air felt at the lint trap opening during a cycle"
  - "The dryer may have been running with a vent that hasn't been cleaned recently"
  - "Drying performance had already been getting slower before heat stopped entirely"
causes:
  - title: "Blown thermal fuse"
    description: "This safety fuse cuts power to the heating circuit if the dryer overheats — most often because a clogged exhaust vent trapped heat inside. It doesn't reset itself and must be replaced."
  - title: "Tripped breaker (electric dryers)"
    description: "Electric dryers run on a 240V double-pole breaker; if only one pole trips, the drum motor (120V) can keep running while the heating element (240V) loses power."
  - title: "Closed or partially closed gas valve (gas dryers)"
    description: "If the gas shutoff valve behind the dryer isn't fully open, the motor and drum work normally but the burner never ignites."
  - title: "Failed heating element or igniter"
    description: "Independent of any fuse or supply issue, the heating element (electric) or igniter (gas) itself can simply wear out and fail."
safeChecks:
  - "Check your breaker panel for a tripped or partially tripped double-pole breaker."
  - "Confirm the gas shutoff valve behind the dryer is fully open (gas models)."
  - "Feel for warm air at the lint trap opening partway through a cycle."
  - "Check when the exhaust vent was last cleaned, since a clogged vent is what typically blows the thermal fuse in the first place."
steps:
  - title: "Check power and gas supply"
    description: "Reset any tripped breaker (both poles) or confirm the gas valve is fully open, then run a short test cycle."
  - title: "Clear the full exhaust vent"
    description: "Clean the lint filter and the entire exhaust duct run, including the exterior wall cap — this is essential even if you suspect the fuse, since a new fuse will blow again with a blocked vent."
  - title: "Test the thermal fuse"
    description: "With power disconnected, the thermal fuse (located near the blower housing or heating element, check your manual) can be tested for continuity with a multimeter, or checked by a technician."
  - title: "Replace the fuse if blown"
    description: "This is an inexpensive part, but always pair replacement with a full vent cleaning to prevent immediate recurrence."
dontDo:
  - "Don't replace a blown thermal fuse without also clearing the vent — a blocked vent will simply blow the new fuse again."
  - "Don't use flexible plastic vent hose for the exhaust run — it's a fire hazard and banned in many areas; use rigid or semi-rigid metal duct."
  - "Don't attempt gas valve or burner repairs yourself beyond checking the shutoff valve position."
whenToCallPro:
  - "You've confirmed power/gas supply and cleared the vent, but there's still no heat."
  - "You smell gas near a gas dryer at any point — leave the area and contact your gas utility immediately."
  - "The breaker trips again after being reset."
  - "You're not comfortable testing or replacing the thermal fuse or heating element yourself."
faqs:
  - q: "How often should I clean my dryer's exhaust vent?"
    a: "Once a year for average use is the general guideline, more often for households doing frequent laundry or with a long or bent vent run."
  - q: "Is a clogged vent really a fire risk, or just an efficiency issue?"
    a: "It's a genuine fire risk — lint is highly flammable, and a blocked vent traps heat inside the dryer and duct, which is a leading cause of home dryer fires, not just a drying-performance issue."
  - q: "Can I test the thermal fuse myself?"
    a: "If you're comfortable safely disconnecting power and using a multimeter for a continuity test, yes — otherwise, a technician can test and replace it along with clearing the vent."
