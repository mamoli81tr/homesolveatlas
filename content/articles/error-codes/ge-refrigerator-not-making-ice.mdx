quickAnswer: "The most common causes, in order, are the ice maker's on/off arm or switch accidentally being bumped off, the freezer running too warm to freeze ice properly, and a frozen or blocked water supply line — all checkable without any tools."
symptoms:
  - "No new ice being produced over 24+ hours"
  - "The ice bin is empty or has only old, shrunken cubes"
  - "You hear the fill valve attempt to run but no water enters the mold"
  - "Freezer temperature seems normal for food storage but ice production has stopped"
causes:
  - title: "Ice maker turned off"
    description: "Most ice makers have an on/off switch or arm that's easy to bump accidentally while loading the freezer — this is the single most common cause of 'no ice' complaints."
  - title: "Freezer temperature too warm"
    description: "Ice makers typically need the freezer at 0–5°F (-18 to -15°C) to cycle properly; a freezer running warmer than that for food storage may still be too warm for consistent ice production."
  - title: "Frozen or kinked water supply line"
    description: "The thin water line feeding the ice maker can freeze at a cold spot or get kinked behind the fridge, blocking water from reaching the mold."
  - title: "Clogged water inlet filter"
    description: "Many GE refrigerators have a water filter that, when overdue for replacement, can restrict flow enough to slow or stop ice production."
safeChecks:
  - "Check the ice maker's on/off switch or arm position — the arm should be down (on) in most designs, not raised."
  - "Check the freezer's actual temperature with an appliance thermometer rather than trusting the display alone."
  - "Check when the water filter was last replaced, typically recommended every 6 months."
  - "Listen for the fill valve attempting to run during a normal cycle, which suggests power is reaching the ice maker."
steps:
  - title: "Confirm the ice maker is switched on"
    description: "Check the on/off arm or switch, since this is bumped off more often than people expect while loading groceries."
  - title: "Check and adjust freezer temperature"
    description: "Confirm the freezer reads 0–5°F (-18 to -15°C) with an independent thermometer, adjusting the control if needed and allowing 24 hours for it to stabilize."
  - title: "Inspect the water line"
    description: "Check for visible kinks in the line behind the fridge, and if you suspect freezing at a specific spot, a hair dryer on low heat can carefully thaw it (never use an open flame)."
  - title: "Replace the water filter"
    description: "If it's overdue, replace the water filter, which can restore normal flow rate to the ice maker."
dontDo:
  - "Don't force the ice maker's arm or reset button repeatedly if it's not responding — check the freezer temperature and water supply first."
  - "Don't use an open flame or excessive heat source to thaw a frozen water line — a hair dryer on low, held at a safe distance, is the safer approach."
  - "Don't ignore a freezer running warmer than it should be, since that affects general food safety too, not just ice production."
