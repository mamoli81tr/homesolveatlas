quickAnswer: "4C (shown as 4E on some models) means the washer isn't sensing enough water flowing in. It's almost always a water supply problem — a closed valve, kinked hose, or clogged inlet filter — rather than an internal fault."
symptoms:
  - "Cycle pauses shortly after starting with '4C' or '4E' on the display"
  - "Little or no water audible entering the drum"
  - "Error appears repeatedly even after restarting the cycle"
  - "Machine was recently moved, installed, or had water supply work done nearby"
causes:
  - title: "Water supply valve not fully open"
    description: "The most common cause — the hot or cold shutoff valve behind the machine is only partially open or was closed for other plumbing work and not fully reopened."
  - title: "Kinked or crushed inlet hose"
    description: "The hoses connecting the wall valves to the machine can kink if the washer was pushed back against the wall without checking hose routing."
  - title: "Clogged inlet filter screens"
    description: "Small mesh filters sit where the hoses connect to the back of the machine. Sediment or debris from the water line can block them over time."
  - title: "Low household water pressure"
    description: "Unusually low incoming water pressure, sometimes temporary during peak usage times or municipal work, can trigger the same code."
safeChecks:
  - "Confirm both hot and cold water shutoff valves behind the machine are turned fully open."
  - "Check both inlet hoses for kinks or tight bends, especially if the machine was recently moved."
  - "Run a nearby cold tap to confirm normal household water pressure."
  - "Unplug the machine before touching any hose connections at the back."
steps:
  - title: "Check the shutoff valves"
    description: "Locate the two valves behind the washer (usually red for hot, blue for cold) and confirm they're turned fully counter-clockwise to open."
  - title: "Straighten the inlet hoses"
    description: "Pull the machine out slightly and check both hoses for kinks between the wall and the machine's back panel."
  - title: "Clean the inlet filter screens"
    description: "With water supply off, unscrew the hoses from the back of the machine and gently clean the small mesh filters inside each connection point with a toothbrush."
  - title: "Reconnect and test"
    description: "Reattach the hoses firmly, turn the water supply back on, and start a short cycle to confirm the error clears."
dontDo:
  - "Don't force a stuck shutoff valve — a seized valve should be checked or replaced by a plumber rather than forced."
  - "Don't run the washer repeatedly on the same error hoping it resolves itself — this can trigger additional fault codes."
  - "Don't remove or bypass the inlet filter screens permanently — they protect the water valve solenoids from debris."
whenToCallPro:
  - "Water pressure is confirmed normal and the error persists after cleaning the filters and hoses."
  - "You can hear the water valve clicking but water still isn't entering the drum."
  - "The shutoff valve itself appears to be leaking or won't turn."
  - "This is a new installation and the error appeared from the very first cycle."
faqs:
  - q: "Is 4C the same as 4E on Samsung washers?"
    a: "Yes — different Samsung model lines display the same water-supply-detection fault as either 4C or 4E."
  - q: "Can I still do laundry while this error appears?"
    a: "Not until it's resolved — the machine won't complete a cycle without confirming adequate water flow, both as a safety and a wash-quality measure."
  - q: "Does this error mean the water inlet valve solenoid is broken?"
    a: "It's possible but far less likely than a supply-side issue. Rule out the shutoff valves, hoses, and filters first before assuming a part failure."

safetyWarning: "Turn off the water supply valves before disconnecting any hose, and have a towel ready to catch residual water. If a valve is seized or leaking, involve a licensed plumber rather than forcing it."
