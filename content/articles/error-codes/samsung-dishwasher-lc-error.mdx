quickAnswer: "LC means Samsung's leak-detection float switch has sensed water pooling in the base pan and stopped the cycle to prevent flooding. It's the same underlying concept as similar leak-lockout codes on other brands — find and clear the source of that water, then reset."
symptoms:
  - "'LC' or 'LC1' displayed and the dishwasher stops mid-cycle or won't start"
  - "Water visible in the base of the unit when the lower kick plate is removed"
  - "The error returns quickly after being cleared"
  - "The dishwasher ran normally before this specific cycle"
causes:
  - title: "Residual water from a prior small leak or overflow"
    description: "Even a leak that has already stopped can leave enough standing water in the base pan to trigger the float switch on the next cycle."
  - title: "A loose or dripping hose connection"
    description: "Fill or drain hose fittings inside the unit can loosen slightly over time or with vibration, dripping into the base pan during use."
  - title: "Excess suds from the wrong detergent"
    description: "Regular dish soap or too much detergent produces excess foam that can escape the tub and drip down to the base, triggering the same sensor as an actual leak."
  - title: "A cracked or misaligned base pan"
    description: "Less commonly, the pan itself can crack or sit unlevel, allowing normal condensation to pool instead of draining away."
safeChecks:
  - "Turn off power at the breaker before removing the lower kick plate to access the base pan."
  - "Look for standing water once the panel is off, and check whether it's actively dripping from somewhere or just residual."
  - "Confirm you're using dishwasher-specific detergent, not liquid dish soap."
  - "Check visible hose connections at the front of the unit for drips."
steps:
  - title: "Cut power and access the base pan"
    description: "Turn off the breaker, then remove the toe-kick panel at the bottom front of the dishwasher."
  - title: "Dry the base pan fully"
    description: "Use a towel or small wet/dry vacuum to remove all standing water so the float switch can reset to its normal position."
  - title: "Watch for an active drip during a short test run"
    description: "With the panel still off, run a short cycle and observe for a few minutes to see if water is actively dripping from a hose or fitting."
  - title: "Address the source and reset"
    description: "Tighten a loose connection if found, switch to proper dishwasher detergent if suds were the cause, then restore power and run a normal cycle to confirm the error clears."
dontDo:
  - "Don't keep restarting the cycle without drying the base pan first — the float switch needs the water actually cleared to reset."
  - "Don't use regular dish soap in a dishwasher — its foam can trigger this exact error and risks overflow."
  - "Don't overtighten plastic hose fittings when checking connections, which can crack them and create a new leak."
